# Blind source and decision plan: JST term spread and four macro outcomes

## Distinct question and custody

This prospective family asks whether the observed long-minus-short nominal interest-rate spread predicts subsequent five-year macro outcomes in the JST R6 advanced-economy panel. Its four outcomes are real GDP-per-person growth, CPI inflation, investment-to-GDP share change, and unemployment-rate change. The fixed eras are 1950–1970, 1975–1995, and 2000–2020. There are twelve cells, but **one substantive family**: outcomes share countries, a source, and macro shocks. Nothing here identifies a monetary-policy intervention or contributes policy, school, position, featured, or causal credit. It does not reuse the pre-1914 broad-money/inflation estimate, pre-1980 loan/crisis test, 1929–1933 trade comparison, or 1929 credit/severity family.

The first registration commit adds all twelve YAML specifications, twelve steelmans, this plan, and `scripts/run_jst_term_spread_macro_2026.py`. The first committed specs must validate against the schema in that same commit. Before that commit, only publisher documentation and the local Parquet schema (column names/types) were inspected. No selected interest-rate or outcome values or their null masks were read. The runner verifies that every registration file remains byte-identical to its first commit. It then reads only country/year keys and Parquet metadata to make twelve separate metadata-only source locks, each committed in fixed cell order. Its measurement path cannot open selected values until all twelve locks are committed. Results and evidence packets first appear in a still-later single commit.

The sole source is the locally held JST R6 `jst_macrohistory@2026-05-05T195309Z.parquet`, SHA-256 `4219d53e595e50b05430b5e4edd91f5f9ebff3987e3d0b6f21d2f2f50bc03a6f`, recorded in `data/manifests/fetch_run_2026-05-05T200056Z.yaml`. The publisher source is <https://www.macrohistory.net/app/download/9834512569/JSTdatasetR6.xlsx>; documentation is <https://www.macrohistory.net/database/>. It describes 18 advanced economies, the `stir` and `ltrate` nominal percentage rates, real GDP per capita, CPI, investment-to-GDP ratio, and unemployment rate. Cite Jordà, Schularick and Taylor (2017). CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 applies: the raw source and country-level values stay local, and exact replay requires a lawfully held exact-hash copy.

## Fixed measurements and gates

The countries are AUS, BEL, CAN, CHE, DEU, DNK, ESP, FIN, FRA, GBR, IRL, ITA, JPN, NLD, NOR, PRT, SWE, and USA. Each era has four anchors: 1950/1955/1960/1965; 1975/1980/1985/1990; 2000/2005/2010/2015. Each anchor requires its t+5 endpoint. The source-key roster is 18 countries × 15 unique endpoint years = 270 keys. The era intervals do not overlap in their interiors; adjacent five-year intervals share one endpoint. No substitute years, countries, vintages, horizon, or outcome columns are permitted.

The exposure is `ltrate[t] - stir[t]` in percentage points. At each anchor, the four distinct responses over t to t+5 are: `20*log(rgdpmad[t+5]/rgdpmad[t])` annualized log percentage points; `20*log(cpi[t+5]/cpi[t])` annualized log percentage points; `100*(iy[t+5]-iy[t])` investment-share percentage points, requiring both ratios in [0,1]; and `unemp[t+5]-unemp[t]` unemployment percentage points, requiring both rates in [0,100]. Outcome-specific complete cases require finite exposure and endpoint values; real GDP and CPI endpoints must be positive. No imputation, trimming, or winsorization is allowed. The predicted sign is positive for the first three and negative for unemployment. Nominal term spreads can reflect inflation expectations and risk, so all tests are predictive associations only.

For each cell, retain countries with at least three complete anchors. Require at least 12 retained countries, 48 country-anchors, and ten countries per anchor. Fit one OLS slope with country and fixed-anchor effects. The country-cluster CR1 variance uses G/(G−1) × (N−1)/(N−K) and a two-sided t test with G−1 degrees of freedom. Fail closed on rank deficiency, nonfinite/nonpositive variance, residualized exposure SD <0.5 pp, residualized outcome SD <0.1 pp, any country with >25% of residualized exposure sum of squares, or an unestimable country-deletion slope. The family always contains twelve hypotheses: Holm adjustment treats gated cells as p=1. With gates passed, support requires a slope of at least 0.25 outcome pp per spread pp in the predicted direction, family-adjusted p<=0.05, and every country-deletion slope in that direction. Refutation applies the same strength and stability criteria in the opposite direction. Other valid cells are PARTIAL; any failed gate is INCONCLUSIVE. No post-result change to this rule or sample can restore blind credit.

## Mechanical sequence

1. Commit the 26 registration files in one commit. Validate all twelve specs before this commit and verify the committed schema and byte identities afterward.
2. Run `python3 scripts/run_jst_term_spread_macro_2026.py plan --source-parquet <local-exact-JST-R6-parquet>`; this checks only schema, hash, and source keys.
3. For each of the twelve IDs in runner order, run `source-lock --id <id> --source-parquet <same-file>` and commit **only that one** metadata JSON. The public evidence gate requires one lock file per commit.
4. Run `run --source-parquet <same-file>`, inspect all gates and family-adjusted verdicts, and commit all twelve aggregate packet directories together. Do not commit raw values or country-level slices.
5. Run `verify --source-parquet <same-file>` for exact replay, regenerate private/public indexes only for checking, and report the independently checked public delta. Keep those generated indexes uncommitted for the consolidated branch to regenerate after merge.
