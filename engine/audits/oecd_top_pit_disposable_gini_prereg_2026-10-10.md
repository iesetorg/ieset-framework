# Top statutory personal income tax and disposable-income Gini: prospective descriptive registration

This one-test study fixes the exposure, OECD IDD Gini selector, exact country/year roster, estimator, and verdict gates **before selected Gini values, null masks, or observation statuses are opened**. Tax values, treatment-only variation, outcome schema and categorical/time keys, hashes/manifests, and prior hypothesis text were inspected. An official OECD search snippet exposed numbers for a **different** IDD working-age poverty measure; that proposed poverty pair was discarded. It did not expose Gini values. There is no claim of global archive blindness.

## Frozen design and treatment-only screen

The key roster is `data/pinned_evidence/oecd_top_pit_disposable_gini_forward_2026_key_roster.csv`, SHA-256 `169effde75deb7226ff45b4d00d0e438a2b39b9d4bd79a38814be35438983caf`. It has exactly 312 unique pairs in 30 countries, sorted by treatment year and country. A candidate needs PIT keys at t−1,t and the exact Gini selector's **keys** at t,t+1; t is 2007–2018. Countries need at least five candidate years. Key presence is an upper bound, not proof of a measured Gini.

Tax-rate endpoints must be finite and in [0,100] percent before outcome access. France 2013 is 122.796992% in the pinned exposure file; the invalid endpoint and both annual differences touching it are excluded, with no cap or replacement. Freeze symmetric categories: **hike** if ΔPIT ≥ +1 percentage point, **cut** if ΔPIT ≤ −1, **stable** if |ΔPIT| < 1. Large otherwise valid reforms remain. The key roster contains 31 hike years in 16 countries, 29 cut years in 17 countries, and 252 stable years. Residualizing the hike indicator on the cut indicator, country and treatment-year effects leaves the top 1% of rows with 13.0% of squared signal, the top country with 10.9%, and the top year with 23.1%. No Gini value or missingness informed this screen.

After a **separate committed source lock**, read only selected Gini values and `OBS_STATUS`; accept finite endpoints in [0,1] with status A at t and t+1. Retain countries with at least five complete pairs. No interpolation, alternative definitions, nearest-year joins, outcome cap, or substitution.

Fit one prespecified OLS model:

`Gini(c,t+1)-Gini(c,t) = country_c + year_t + β_hike·1[ΔPIT≥+1] + β_cut·1[ΔPIT≤−1] + error(c,t)`.

The stable-rate group is the reference; the cut coefficient is nuisance only. Gini at t is already the subtraction endpoint and can be affected by the year-t rate change, so it is **not** added again as a baseline covariate. This fixes a narrow subsequent-change association and leaves regression-to-mean and starting-inequality confounding risks. Country-cluster CR1 standard errors and two-sided Student t with G−1 degrees of freedom govern the hike test. Require full design rank and finite positive cluster uncertainty. Complete-case floors are at least 220 rows, 25 countries, five pairs per retained country, 20 hike rows in 12 countries, and 15 cut rows in 10 countries. Partialled-hike concentration must be at most 20% in the top 1% of rows, 20% in the top country, and 30% in the top year after complete-case filtering.

If any custody, chronology, sample, event, rank, concentration, or uncertainty gate fails, return **INCONCLUSIVE**. Otherwise **SUPPORTED** requires β_hike ≤ −0.005 Gini units and two-sided p ≤ 0.005; **REFUTED** requires β_hike ≥ +0.005 at the same p; every other estimable result is **PARTIAL**. This is a one-test exploratory threshold, not a global multiplicity correction.

The top statutory wage-income rate is not an effective household tax. Bracket thresholds, deductions, capital income, transfers, contemporaneous reforms, macro shocks, income timing, and survey revisions can move the Gini. The Gini is broad and may not respond much to a top-rate change. The outcome window ends in 2019, before pandemic effects. This is descriptive calibration only; all verdicts have zero causal, policy, school, position, and featured credit.
