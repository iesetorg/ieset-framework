# Maastricht scope and earliest national drift audit — 10 October 2026

## Earliest supported coverage

The current authored corpus has **717 movement** files. Before this repair, 711 were included as national movements, five were marked supranational, and one was subnational; afterward the scope counts are 710, six, and one. Its earliest national movement is the narrowly coded French provincial-privilege abolition in **1789**. The **3,409 policy** files also begin in 1789; neither directory has a dated record before that year. The national drift generator derives its year grid from authored national movement starts and documented axis attribution years. Its existing `1789–2026` span is therefore the full current corpus, not a reason to fill earlier dates with observed zeroes. The movement and policy schemas have a 1789 minimum; extending beyond it would need an independently sourced, dated, axis-reviewed national record and a schema change.

The earliest **statist composite** step remains the UK's 1846 Corn Law movement. France's narrow 1789 rule-of-law step is outside that composite. The chart masks each country's pre-coding years; zero-filled alignment in the generated file is not an observed policy level. State, municipal, and supranational movements are excluded from national totals.

## Treaty-wide record incorrectly included in country scores

`maastricht_convergence_criteria_1992` was the only multi-country record defaulting to national scope. Its shared 1992 coding placed `monetary.central_bank_independence + strong` and `fiscal.spending_level - moderate` into **11** country trajectories. That is unsupported as a common national decision year. [EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/LSU/?uri=CELEX:11992M/TXT) dates the Maastricht Treaty's signature to **7 February 1992** and entry into force to **1 November 1993**. The [ECB's EMU chronology](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/ecb/history-arts-culture/history/emu/html/index.en.html) separates the **1997** Stability and Growth Pact, the ECB's **1 June 1998** establishment, and the **1 January 1999** single-monetary-policy launch. It names the initial 11 euro participants, including **Luxembourg**, while **Greece joined in 2001**. The existing 11-country list included Greece and omitted Luxembourg. The [EUR-Lex 1994 accession treaty](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/treaty/acc_1994/art_2/sign/eng) identifies the 12 pre-accession member states, which include Denmark and the United Kingdom but not Austria, Finland, or Sweden.

The movement is now explicitly **supranational**, with its 12 treaty signatories and distinct signature/effective dates. Its three linked policy records remain visible but are marked supranational and dated to the Treaty (1992 signature/1993 force), Pact (1997 adoption), and ECB establishment (1998), respectively. Their country lists describe the corresponding treaty signatories, 1997 EU members, and initial euro participants; they do not establish simultaneous domestic implementation. Existing policy-axis and inferred-hypothesis labels are not promoted as measured national effects. Country-specific central-bank legislation and fiscal changes still require their own source and dates before any new national step.

## Exact derived-map effect

An isolated regeneration compared with the parent commit changes **only** AUT, BEL, DEU, ESP, FIN, FRA, GRC, IRL, ITA, NLD, and PRT. Each loses the treaty movement marker. For each of those countries in each of the **35 years 1992–2026**, the central-bank-independence axis falls by **3**, the spending-level axis rises by **2**, and the statist composite rises by **5** relative to the previous constructed series: **385 country-years** changed. Greece's first coded central-bank-independence year becomes 1996 rather than 1992; the Netherlands' becomes 1999; Ireland has no remaining signed step on that axis. No other axis, country, country-level composite start, or year changes. The regenerated range remains **1789–2026**, with **145 countries** and **21 axes**.

This is a correction of coding scope, not a measured change in fiscal or monetary policy. It changes no hypothesis verdict or public graduation count. The source files and focused regression test are committed separately from generated indexes so the consolidated branch can regenerate all derived artifacts together.
