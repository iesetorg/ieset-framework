# Pre-1789 policy context and country-scope audit — 10 October 2026

## Decision

The authored movement corpus has no defensible pre-1789 national signed-axis step. `scripts/compute_country_drift.py --check` still reports 145 countries and a numeric 1789–2026 grid. The policy schema floor is moved only to 1788 to admit the French civil-status edict as a candidate, unscored policy record. No movement, axis, hypothesis verdict, or generated drift value is changed in this family.

## French non-Catholic civil-status edict

Added `fr_non_catholic_civil_status_edict_1787_1788` as a national royal *instrument* for France, with `timeframe.start: 1788` and no `enacted_date` or `effective_date`. The [original edict](https://museeprotestant.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/01/Edit-du-Roi-de-novembre-1787-Musee-protestant.pdf) dates itself only to November 1787. The [BnF manuscript catalogue](https://ccfr.bnf.fr/portailccfr/ark%3A/16871/004D41440037) records registration in Parlement on **29 January 1788**. The exact day is a registration date, not established as one effective date for the entire kingdom. Article XXI of the original edict expressly computes the period for regularising earlier marriages from publication and registration in the court governing a person's domicile. The 1788 timeline year therefore marks the first specifically dated registration found, without claiming uniform operation.

Articles I–IV and XXI–XXVIII of the edict cover civil marriage and birth/death records, limited property and professional rights, and related procedures while retaining Catholic public worship alone and restrictions on non-Catholic corporate association and offices. The [French National Assembly chronology](https://www.assemblee-nationale.fr/histoire/eglise-etat/chronologie.asp) confirms civil status without general freedom of worship or access to public office. The record has no `axes_moved`, `enacted_by`, or inferred hypothesis links and does not create a numeric drift step or an outcome claim.

## Earlier Great Britain candidate: context, not a country policy row

The [Parliament's original Articles of Union](https://www.parliament.uk/globalassets/documents/heritage/articlesofunion.pdf), Articles I, III, IV and VI, specify that England and Scotland became the Kingdom of **Great Britain** on **1 May 1707**, with one Parliament, internal trade and navigation rights, and common customs and trade rules with express exceptions. [UK Parliament's historical account](https://www.parliament.uk/about/living-heritage/evolutionofparliament/legislativescrutiny/act-of-union-1707/overview/the-articles-constitution-and-trade/) confirms that this Great Britain included England, Scotland and Wales; Ireland remained outside it. The trade provisions include harmonised restrictions as well as access, so no one-direction market axis follows from the text.

The present drift/browser country mapping displays `GBR` as “United Kingdom.” A `GBR` policy row dated 1707 would imply the later UK, including Ireland, and would mix a new country denominator with a within-country change. This candidate is retained here as sourced timeline context until the policy schema and browser can identify the historical polity without mislabelling its territory. It is not used to extend the signed country series.

## Deferred 1784 Commutation Act

The available official statute and parliamentary history identify the 1784 tea-duty reduction alongside replacement house/window and other revenue provisions, but this pass did not verify the original instrument's exact legally operative day and territorial scope for every channel from a primary text. The bundled tax changes also do not support one signed drift direction. No policy row or axis score is entered pending that source work.
