# OECD combined statutory CIT change to BERD and net FDI: prospective two-test family

This design was written before opening either selected outcome's `value` column, outcome null pattern, or the WDI FDI `obs_status` column. The preflight inspected exact hashes and manifests, outcome schemas and categorical selectors, country/year keys, OECD CIT **treatment** values, and visible earlier hypothesis text. The two tests are descriptive calibration only, with no school, policy, causal, or featured credit. The Bonferroni correction below is local to this family, not a correction for the visible IESET archive.

## Frozen roster, sample, and model

The key-only roster `data/pinned_evidence/oecd_cit_berd_fdi_family_2026_key_roster.csv` has SHA-256 `2f0318e4d42002772babd3eb1967f90561d1c02ecc1dd7666422745b1900cd01`; it is sorted by case (`berd`, then `fdi`), treatment year, and ISO3. A candidate `t` is 2001–2017 inclusive, requires CIT at `t−1,t` and its exact outcome at `t,t+2`, and belongs to a country with at least five key-only candidate years. The two-year outcome endpoint is 2019 at latest, before pandemic-era observations. A key is only an upper bound on a complete measured pair.

| Test | Key-only pairs / countries | Pre-read CIT changes `|ΔCIT|≥1` pp pairs / countries | Minimum complete pairs / countries | Minimum complete material-change pairs / countries | Meaningful `|β|` |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| BERD/GDP | 565 / 35 | 128 / 32 | 400 / 25 | 60 / 20 | 0.02 pp BERD/GDP per 1 pp CIT |
| Net FDI/GDP | 2,387 / 141 | 382 / 122 | 1,500 / 80 | 180 / 60 | 0.2 pp FDI/GDP per 1 pp CIT |

These meaningful beta thresholds correspond to a 5 pp CIT change with a 0.1 pp BERD/GDP or 1 pp net-FDI/GDP two-year forward difference. They were set from substantive scale before outcome values or missingness, not by searching estimates. Tax changes below 1 pp and exactly zero remain in the regression; the material-change count is an informativeness floor, not a treatment-only sample. There is no imputation, winsorization, alternate lag, country replacement, or window extension.

After the **separate committed source lock**, read outcomes, require finite Eurostat percentages in `[0,100]`, finite WDI FDI percentages with no sign/range clamp, and blank WDI status at both endpoints. Drop countries with fewer than five complete pairs. Define `X(c,t)=CIT(c,t)−CIT(c,t−1)` and `Y(c,t)=Outcome(c,t+2)−Outcome(c,t)`, both in percentage points. Fit exactly one OLS model per outcome:

`Y(c,t) = α_c + λ_t + β X(c,t) + ε(c,t)`.

Use country and treatment-year effects, country-cluster CR1 small-sample correction, and two-sided Student `t(G−1)` inference. Require full rank and finite positive cluster uncertainty. If a frozen complete-case or material-exposure floor fails, or estimation fails, verdict **INCONCLUSIVE**. Otherwise **SUPPORTED** requires negative beta of at least the test's meaningful magnitude and two-sided `p≤0.005`; **REFUTED** requires positive beta of at least that magnitude and the same p cutoff; all other estimable results are **PARTIAL**. Both tests remain in one Bonferroni family at alpha 0.01 even if one is inconclusive.

## Prior work and limits

There is no exact OECD CIT treatment to BERD or net-FDI outcome pairing in the 2,018 canonical hypotheses inspected before registration. The archive does include a legacy CIT-as-outcome tax competition test, 30 direct WDI FDI-outcome specs with other treatments (16 public-visible), two business-R&D-as-treatment screens, the corporate-tax investment placeholder, and a Tax Foundation alias/proxy-to-investment candidate. These related visible studies mean the local family correction is not archive-wide selection adjustment.

Both endpoints divide by GDP, so GDP shocks can create apparent co-movement with tax changes. BERD measures sector of **performance**, not private funding. FDI net flows may capture conduit structures, mergers, and financial volatility rather than physical investment. Statutory CIT is not the effective rate faced by a project. Growth expectations, simultaneous reforms, and global shocks can move tax rates and outcomes together. Country/year effects and temporal ordering do not establish a causal or policy effect. Preserve negative, null, missing, and inconclusive findings with zero school, policy, causal, and featured credit.
