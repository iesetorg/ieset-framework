# OECD ALMP × EPL temporal holdout review — 2026-10-09

The new OECD ALMP × EPL test was registered in commit `b5242ef61c0b1096c0d9637c36e1e60775ff0e43` before selected source magnitudes were read. Its manifest-verified 34-country source slice was committed separately in `2bdbe6de459af7f5b7c633e522ead7b7e8023a50`, and its result in `ed6a80562f7bf30818c76eb443213a970d1370e2`. The pinned-source extraction and full result replay both passed. `scripts/check_preregistration.py` recognizes the spec/run pair as strictly preregistered.

The fixed 2020–21 exposure / 2021–22 outcome design retained 34 countries and 68 country-year pairs, above its frozen 30-country/60-pair floor. The 2019 OECD EPR Version 1 index moderates the change in OECD SOCX public active-labour-market spending as a percentage of GDP. The primary interaction coefficient was **−0.6641** (country-clustered SE **0.2323**, two-sided t p **0.00731**). Because the registered prediction was a *positive* interaction, the narrow measured-association verdict is **REFUTED** under the prespecified 0.01 cutoff.

An independent least-squares recalculation from the committed source slice reproduced the interaction coefficient to floating-point precision. The design had rank four and a standardized predictor condition number of 1.37. A post-result, non-dispositive leave-one-country-out check kept the interaction negative in 34 of 34 refits; 28 of 34 remained below p=0.01. This check was not part of the preregistered verdict and does not change it.

The result is a COVID-era descriptive stress test, **not** evidence that ALMP spending caused unemployment or that stricter EPL improved programme effectiveness. The [OECD SOCX database](https://www.oecd.org/en/data/datasets/social-expenditure-database-socx.html) measures programme spending, while the [OECD EPL indicators](https://www.oecd.org/en/data/datasets/oecd-indicators-of-employment-protection.html) measure legal protections. The [OECD Employment Outlook 2021](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/oecd-employment-outlook-2021_5a700c4b-en/full-report/labour-market-developments-the-unfolding-covid-19-crisis_05296554.html) explains that unemployment missed substantial pandemic labour slack, and [Education at a Glance 2021](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/education-at-a-glance-2021_b35a14e5-en/full-report/component-10.html) documents exceptional low-education labour-market and inactivity changes. Pandemic policy responses, labour-force exits, reopening, and GDP-denominator changes may drive the observed interaction. It earns no named-policy, causal, or school-score credit and does not repair the old 1996–2019 PARTIAL result.
