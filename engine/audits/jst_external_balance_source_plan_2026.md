# Blind source and decision plan: JST current-account deficit and external adjustment

## Scientific question and provenance

This prospective family asks whether a larger nominal current-account deficit relative to GDP predicts external stress over the next five years in the 18 advanced economies of JST R6. The distinct outcomes are local-currency depreciation against the USD, CPI inflation, real GDP per-person growth, unemployment-rate change, and an incident systemic financial crisis. The sign predictions for a larger deficit are positive, positive, negative, positive, and positive, respectively. The first two eras (1950–1970 and 1975–1995) have all five outcomes. The 2000–2020 era omits the USD-exchange-rate cell because shared euro exchange rates after 1999 would give many country observations one currency trajectory. That makes **fourteen cells in one substantive family**. Outcomes and eras are correlated through countries, source, and shocks; they are not fourteen independent replications. This is a descriptive predictive test, not an identified effect of external borrowing or any policy.

The first registration commit adds fourteen YAML specs, fourteen steelmans, this plan, and `scripts/run_jst_external_balance_2026.py` together. Their first committed bytes and the schema blob in that commit are authoritative. Before registration, only publisher documentation and local Parquet column names/types were read. No selected `ca`, `gdp`, `xrusd`, `cpi`, `rgdpmad`, `unemp`, or `crisisjst` values or null masks were inspected. The runner freezes the commit and validates all fourteen first-commit specs against that same committed schema. It reads only source keys and metadata for fourteen separately committed, metadata-only source locks. Only after all locks are committed may it read selected measurements. The raw JST table, selected rows, and country-level effects remain local and are never committed.

The source is the locally held `jst_macrohistory@2026-05-05T195309Z.parquet`, SHA-256 `4219d53e595e50b05430b5e4edd91f5f9ebff3987e3d0b6f21d2f2f50bc03a6f`, recorded in `data/manifests/fetch_run_2026-05-05T200056Z.yaml`. Publisher source: <https://www.macrohistory.net/app/download/9834512569/JSTdatasetR6.xlsx>. [Official database and variable definitions](https://www.macrohistory.net/database/) identify current account and GDP as nominal local-currency values and USD exchange rate as local currency per USD; they cover the other outcome series and the 18 advanced economies. The publisher's [crisis chronology](https://www.macrohistory.net/app/download/9844625569/JSTcrisis_chronology.pdf) defines `crisisJST` as a first-year onset indicator. Cite Jordà, Schularick and Taylor (2017). The source is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0; lawful exact-hash local custody is needed for replay.

## Fixed keys, measurements, and decision

The countries are AUS, BEL, CAN, CHE, DEU, DNK, ESP, FIN, FRA, GBR, IRL, ITA, JPN, NLD, NOR, PRT, SWE, and USA. The four anchors per era are 1950/1955/1960/1965, 1975/1980/1985/1990, and 2000/2005/2010/2015. For each anchor, the fixed window is t through t+5. The crisis event uses t+1 through t+5 inclusive, so adjacent crisis windows are disjoint; continuous response intervals share endpoints but not interiors. Source keys comprise all annual country-years 1950–1970, 1975–1995, and 2000–2020: 63 years × 18 = 1,134 keys. Every key must be present once. No replacement vintage, country, year, horizon, event convention, or outcome is allowed.

At anchor t, exposure is `-100*ca[t]/gdp[t]`, so positive means a deficit as percent of GDP; current account must be finite and GDP strictly positive. USD depreciation, CPI inflation, and real-GDP-per-person growth are `20*log(endpoint/start)` in annualized log percentage points, requiring positive finite endpoints. Unemployment change is `unemp[t+5]-unemp[t]` in percentage points, requiring finite rates in [0,100]. Crisis-onset response is 100 if any `crisisjst` value equals 1 during t+1 through t+5 inclusive, else 0; all five source codes must be exactly 0/1. Each cell uses its own complete cases. No imputation, trimming, or winsorization is allowed.

Retain countries with at least three of four complete anchors. Require at least 12 retained countries, 48 country-anchors, and 10 countries per anchor. The crisis cell also requires at least six incident and twenty nonincident retained intervals. Estimate one two-way fixed-effect OLS slope per cell, with country and anchor intercepts. For the crisis event this is an explicitly labeled linear probability model in percentage-point outcome units. The country-cluster CR1 variance uses G/(G−1) × (N−1)/(N−K), with two-sided t inference on G−1 degrees of freedom. Fail closed on rank deficiency, nonfinite/nonpositive variance, residualized deficit SD <0.5 pp, residualized outcome SD <0.1 pp, any one country with >25% of residualized deficit sum of squares, or an unestimable country-deletion slope. The fourteen-cell Holm correction includes gated cells as p=1.

After gates pass, SUPPORTED requires beta of at least 0.25 outcome pp per one pp deficit in the predicted direction, Holm-adjusted p<=0.05, and every country-deletion slope in that direction. REFUTED uses the same magnitude, multiplicity, and deletion requirements in the opposite direction. Other valid cells are PARTIAL; any failed gate is INCONCLUSIVE. Source and sample failures are not permission to change the rule. Neither a supported nor refuted descriptive cell grants causal, policy, school, position, or featured credit.

This is distinct from the prior pre-1914 money/inflation test, interwar trade contraction, 1929 credit/severity family, pre-1980 loan/crisis-onset test, and the term-spread predictor family. The term-spread family uses an interest-rate exposure; this test uses current-account deficit. It also differs from the credit-gap/current-account interaction in the existing corpus, which conditions an unemployment outcome on credit stress rather than estimating this fixed JST external-balance family. The 2000–2020 exchange-rate exclusion is design-driven by the known shared euro currency, not by observed outcome data.
