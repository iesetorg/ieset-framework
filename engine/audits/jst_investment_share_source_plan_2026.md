# Blind source and decision plan: JST investment share and future living standards

## Scientific question and provenance

This prospective family asks whether a larger observed investment-to-GDP ratio at a fixed anchor predicts subsequent material outcomes across 18 advanced economies in JST R6. Four outcome series are real GDP per-person growth, real consumption per-person growth, CPI-deflated wage-index growth, and unemployment-rate change. The predicted signs are positive, positive, positive, and negative. The five disjoint historical eras are 1890–1910, 1920–1940, 1950–1970, 1975–1995, and 2000–2020. Four anchors per era and four outcomes yield **twenty cells in one substantive family**, with Holm correction over all twenty. The outcomes are measured separately but correlated, especially GDP and consumption; countries, source, and historical shocks overlap. Neither cells nor eras are independent replications. This is descriptive prediction, not a causal investment or policy estimate.

The first registration commit adds twenty schema-valid YAML specs, twenty steelmans, this plan, and `scripts/run_jst_investment_share_2026.py` together. Their first committed bytes and the schema blob in that commit are authoritative. Before registration, only publisher documentation, source file hash from the prior JST custody, and local Parquet column names/types were read. No selected `iy`, `rgdpmad`, `rconsbarro`, `wage`, `cpi`, or `unemp` values or null masks were inspected for this family. Prior term-spread work did examine aggregate `iy` changes as an outcome; that previous aggregate finding was visible, so this family makes no claim of complete blindness to all investment-related results. The runner validates every first-commit spec against the schema blob from the registration commit. It reads source keys and metadata only for twenty separately committed metadata source locks. Selected measurements may be opened only after all source locks are committed. Raw JST rows and country-level effects remain local.

## Fixed keys, measurements, and decision

The countries are AUS, BEL, CAN, CHE, DEU, DNK, ESP, FIN, FRA, GBR, IRL, ITA, JPN, NLD, NOR, PRT, SWE, and USA. Era anchors are 1890/1895/1900/1905, 1920/1925/1930/1935, 1950/1955/1960/1965, 1975/1980/1985/1990, and 2000/2005/2010/2015. Each fixed response window is t through t+5. The source-key roster is the 25 unique anchor or endpoint years times 18 countries = 450 country-years, each present exactly once. No alternative era, country, vintage, horizon, endpoint, or outcome is allowed. Pre-1914 and interwar coverage or war disruption may fail gates and remain visible as INCONCLUSIVE.

At anchor t exposure is `100*iy[t]`, in investment-share percentage points of GDP, requiring finite iy in [0,1]. Real GDP per-person and consumption per-person responses are `20*log(endpoint/start)` annualized log percentage points, with positive finite endpoints. CPI-deflated wage-index response is `20*log((wage[t+5]/cpi[t+5])/(wage[t]/cpi[t]))`, with all four endpoint values positive and finite. Unemployment-rate response is `unemp[t+5]-unemp[t]`, with finite endpoints in [0,100]. Each cell uses its own complete cases. No imputation, trimming, or winsorization.

Retain only countries with at least three of four complete anchors. Require at least 12 countries, 48 country-anchors, and 10 countries per anchor. Estimate one two-way country-and-anchor fixed-effect OLS slope per cell. Country-cluster CR1 variance uses G/(G−1) × (N−1)/(N−K), with two-sided t inference on G−1 degrees of freedom. Fail closed on rank deficiency, nonfinite/nonpositive variance, residualized investment-share SD <0.5 pp, residualized outcome SD <0.1 pp, any country with >25% of residualized investment-share sum of squares, or an unestimable country-deletion slope. The fixed twenty-cell Holm correction includes gated cells as p=1.

Once gates pass, SUPPORTED requires beta at least 0.25 outcome pp per one pp investment share in the predicted direction, Holm-adjusted p<=0.05, and every country-deletion slope in that direction. REFUTED uses the same magnitude, multiplicity, and deletion rules in the opposite direction. Other valid cells are PARTIAL; any gate failure is INCONCLUSIVE. There is no causal, policy, school, position, or featured credit. Higher investment share can reflect expected growth or shocks and is mechanically related to the GDP denominator, so no capital-policy claim follows.

This differs from the earlier term-spread family, which used investment-share change as an outcome, and from the current-account-deficit and interwar-credit families, which use different exposures and questions. GDP and consumption are distinct source series and both are retained to see whether aggregate production and household consumption agree; they are correlated and the twenty-cell family adjustment explicitly covers them. Historical eras represent separate regimes but share countries and source construction.
