# Blind source and decision plan: JST mortgage-loan share, housing prices, and crisis onset

## Scientific question and provenance

This prospective family asks whether mortgage loans as a share of all nonfinancial private loans at a fixed anchor predict subsequent real house-price growth and incident systemic financial crisis. The two outcomes are distinct: an index-based price response and a first-year crisis-onset indicator. They share countries and historical financial shocks and are not independent replications. The four disjoint eras are 1890–1910, 1950–1970, 1975–1995, and 2000–2020, with four anchors each. This yields **eight cells in one substantive family** and a fixed eight-test Holm correction. It differs from the prior 1929 aggregate-credit exposure, the interest-rate-spread and current-account families, and the investment-share living-standards family. It is descriptive prediction, not an identified mortgage-lending or housing-policy effect.

The first registration commit adds eight schema-valid YAML specs, eight steelmans, this plan, and `scripts/run_jst_mortgage_share_2026.py` together. The runner validates their first-commit bytes against the schema blob from that same commit. Before registration, only publisher documentation and the local Parquet schema/column types were read. No selected `tmort`, `tloans`, `hpnom`, `cpi`, or `crisisjst` values or null masks were inspected. The exact source hash is from prior JST custody. After first registration, only country/year keys, source hash, and field types are inspected while committing eight metadata-only source locks, one file per commit. Selected measurements may be opened only after the final lock is committed. Raw JST rows and country-level effects remain local.

Cite Jordà, Schularick and Taylor (2017). The dataset is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0; exact-hash local lawful custody is required for replay, and no raw/country-level values will be committed.

## Fixed keys, measurements, and decision

The countries are AUS, BEL, CAN, CHE, DEU, DNK, ESP, FIN, FRA, GBR, IRL, ITA, JPN, NLD, NOR, PRT, SWE, and USA. Anchors are 1890/1895/1900/1905, 1950/1955/1960/1965, 1975/1980/1985/1990, and 2000/2005/2010/2015. Every annual source year in the four 21-year eras must have exactly one row for all 18 countries: 84 × 18 = 1,512 keys. The real-price response uses endpoints t and t+5. The incident crisis event is 1 if any crisisJST code equals 1 from t+1 through t+5 inclusive, else 0; every included annual code must be exactly 0 or 1. Those crisis windows are non-overlapping within an era. No substitute countries, years, vintage, event code, or outcome are allowed.

At anchor t, exposure is `100*tmort[t]/tloans[t]` in percentage points of private lending. Require both stocks finite, tloans>0, and ratio in [0,1]. Real-house-price response is `20*log((hpnom[t+5]/cpi[t+5])/(hpnom[t]/cpi[t]))` annualized log percentage points; all four index endpoint values must be positive finite. Crisis onset response is 0 or 100 percentage points, requiring five valid future codes. Each cell uses its own complete cases. No imputation, trimming, or winsorization is allowed.

Retain only countries with at least three of four complete anchors. Require at least 12 countries, 48 country-anchors, and 10 countries at each anchor. Crisis onset additionally requires six incident and twenty nonincident retained intervals. Estimate one two-way country-and-anchor fixed-effect OLS slope per cell; the crisis cell is explicitly a linear probability model in percentage-point units. Country-cluster CR1 variance uses G/(G−1) × (N−1)/(N−K), with two-sided t inference on G−1 degrees of freedom. Fail closed on rank deficiency, nonfinite/nonpositive variance, residualized mortgage-share SD <1.0 pp, residualized outcome SD <0.1 pp, any country with >25% of residualized exposure sum of squares, or an unestimable country-deletion slope. The fixed eight-cell Holm correction includes gated cells as p=1.

Once gates pass, SUPPORTED requires beta at least 0.25 outcome pp per one pp mortgage share in the predicted positive direction, Holm-adjusted p<=0.05, and every country-deletion slope positive. REFUTED applies the same magnitude/multiplicity/deletion requirements for a negative slope. Other valid cells are PARTIAL; any failed gate is INCONCLUSIVE. Neither status grants causal, policy, school, position, or featured credit. Historical data quality, changing mortgage institutions, and endogenous house prices are material limits.
