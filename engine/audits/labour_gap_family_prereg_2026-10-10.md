# Labour-gap family: prospective registration

This is a fixed two-test descriptive family. It was prepared from source dimensions, country/year keys, and treatment values only. Selected OECD unemployment values and their missingness were not opened. The exact roster CSV, two hypotheses, steelmen, and guarded runner must be added together in a registration commit. A separate later commit must add the source lock after exact SHA recovery. Only then may the runner open selected outcome values. Both tests remain in the family denominator even if one is inconclusive.

## Fixed questions

1. **Public active labour market spending.** In the 35 named countries and treatment years 2001-2022, does a larger change from t-1 to t in OECD public SOCX active labour market programme spending, percentage points of GDP, predict a smaller change from t to t+1 in the OECD foreign-born minus native-born unemployment-rate gap? SOCX selector: FREQ=A, MEASURE=SOCX, UNIT_MEASURE=PT_B1GQ, EXPEND_SOURCE=ES10, SPENDING_TYPE=_T, PROGRAMME_TYPE=TP60, PRICE_BASE=_Z. Exactly 641 country-year keys across 35 countries pass the pre-value roster rule, with 247 rows/31 countries having absolute spending change >=0.05 GDP percentage point.
2. **Foreign-citizen and stateless arrivals.** In the 26 named European countries and the same years, does a larger change from t-1 to t in Eurostat foreign-country or stateless arrivals per 1,000 January-1 residents predict a larger change from t to t+1 in the same OECD birth-place unemployment gap? Immigration selector: freq=A, citizen=FOR_STLS, agedef=REACH, age=TOTAL, unit=NR, sex=T. Denominator: demo_pjan, freq=A, age=TOTAL, unit=NR, sex=T. The frozen alpha-2 to ISO3 mapping includes EL=GRC and UK=GBR; aggregates and unmatched areas are excluded. Exactly 463 country-year keys/26 countries pass, with 182 rows/25 countries having absolute arrival-rate change >=1 per 1,000 residents.

The OECD outcome selects annual CITIZENSHIP=_Z, MEASURE=UNE_RATE, SEX=_T, EDUCATION_LEV=_Z, UNIT_MEASURE=PT_LF_SUB, and BIRTH_PLACE=FB or NB. UNE_RATE is the percentage of the labour force in the same birth-place subgroup, so the outcome is a percentage-point **foreign-born minus native-born unemployment-rate gap change**. The two birth-place keys must exist at t and t+1; the EU aggregate is excluded. The pinned OECD parquet schema also contains string OBS_STATUS, but embeds no status code labels. Key-only registration does not read that field. After source lock, retain only blank or A (normal) status for each selected FB/NB observation; exclude estimated, provisional, break, imputed, forecast, and other nonnormal codes before forming a gap. [SDMX CL_OBS_STATUS](https://sdmx.org/news/version-2-3-of-cl_obs_status-released/) states that blank status defaults to A normal. OECD's selected vintage has 1,585 birth-place rows, 39 areas including the aggregate, 2000-2024, and zero duplicate area/year/birth-place keys.

The tests have separate fixed rosters. The arrivals roster is European while the ALMP roster also has non-European OECD countries. Their coefficients must not be compared. Their treatment-year key intersection has 433 rows/26 countries under the prespecified five-year rule. The full ALMP roster includes AUS, AUT, BEL, CAN, CHE, CHL, COL, CZE, DEU, DNK, ESP, EST, FIN, FRA, GBR, GRC, HUN, IRL, ISL, ISR, ITA, LTU, LUX, LVA, MEX, NLD, NOR, NZL, POL, PRT, SVK, SVN, SWE, TUR, USA. ## Frozen estimator and verdict

Each study uses one primary OLS coefficient of forward gap change on its treatment change, with country and treatment-year fixed effects and **no baseline-gap covariate**. Uncertainty uses CR1 country-cluster covariance and a two-sided Student t reference with G-1 cluster degrees of freedom. The family alpha is 0.01; the two registered tests use fixed Bonferroni two-sided p<=0.005 each and corresponding 99.5% confidence intervals. No model, control, lag, geography, or endpoint search may change the verdict. A separate full-window model adds the baseline gap as a diagnostic only. Because the response is future minus baseline gap, that adjusted change model is algebraically an ANCOVA of future gap and can be sensitive to regression to the mean. Its sign, p, or magnitude cannot change the primary verdict.

A directional claim requires both that cutoff and a prespecified practical effect of at least **0.1 percentage point of gap change** for an exposure rise of **0.1 GDP percentage point** of ALMP spending or **1 arrival per 1,000 residents**. The ALMP prediction is negative; the arrival prediction is positive. An opposite effect satisfying the same p and magnitude rules refutes the measured direction. Any other estimable primary result is PARTIAL.

Complete-case floors, fixed before outcome access, are at least 300 rows/25 countries plus 80 rows/20 countries with absolute ALMP change >=0.05 GDP percentage point; and at least 220 rows/18 countries plus 60 rows/12 countries with absolute arrival-rate change >=1 per 1,000. Each retained country needs at least five complete treatment years. Nonfinite or out-of-range selected outcome rates are dropped only after source lock. Rank-deficient models or invalid cluster uncertainty are INCONCLUSIVE.

For arrivals, the age definition REACH is primary because Eurostat defines it as age reached during the year and the earlier registered arrival/Gini study used this selector. A mandatory COMPLET sensitivity uses the same frozen primary-key intersection. Its own complete-case and material-exposure floor must pass. A directional SUPPORTED or REFUTED verdict requires the COMPLET coefficient independently to have the same sign, two-sided p<=0.005, and practical magnitude; otherwise an estimable case is PARTIAL. In the pinned exposure source, REACH has Belgium 2013-14 while COMPLET does not; Netherlands 2018 reports 146,740 REACH versus 76,789 COMPLET arrivals despite total age. This is known before outcome access and cannot be selected by fit. If COMPLET cannot be estimated under its frozen floor, the arrival study is INCONCLUSIVE.

For both tests, rerun the fixed primary change-only model after excluding 2022 and after restricting t<=2019. Also report the full-window baseline-adjusted diagnostic. These three estimates are diagnostic only; they cannot create a verdict. They matter because 2022 arrival changes are unusually large across Europe and baseline adjustment can expose regression-to-mean sensitivity. The primary full-window verdict and all sensitivity estimates must be reported. This is descriptive calibration only: zero causal, policy-effect, school-score, position, or featured credit.

## Prior-test boundary

Neither directed pair was found in the strict or legacy hypothesis specifications. Nearest visible cases are public ALMP spending versus aggregate unemployment, ALMP x EPL versus low-education unemployment, Eurostat arrivals versus disposable-income Gini, and EPL versus a tertiary migrant/native **employment** gap. Those do not supply a verdict here. These studies reuse sources and some country-years; they are not data-independent replications or new policy interventions.
