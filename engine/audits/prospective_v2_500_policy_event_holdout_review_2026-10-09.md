# Policy-event and temporal holdout audit for the 500-case v2 queue

Date: 2026-10-09. Scope: selected legacy cases in `prospective_v2_500_review_queue_2026-10-09.json`, with emphasis on named events, national sources, and non-WDI country panels. This is design review only: no v2 specification, registration, estimate, result, or graduation was created. Existing old results were read for leakage checks. Candidate held-out outcome **values** below were not inspected or fitted; coverage, schema, source manifests, and SHA-256 records were inspected.

The old result must be disclosed in every later v2. A new ID and committed, immutable rule must precede estimating any proposed endpoint. The cases below are ranked by a measured, disjoint endpoint and a defensible descriptive estimand, not by the old result or expected conclusion. They cannot inherit the old verdict or a causal label.

## Ranked design leads

### 1. Argentina: post-2025 core disinflation durability — design-ready as descriptive validation

- **Old ID:** `milei_reforms_reduce_argentine_inflation` (queue rank 153). Old spec covers Argentina 2018–2027, but its dispositive monthly headline-CPI windows are October 2024–February 2025 and April–August 2025. Its 2026-05-15 run was `INCONCLUSIVE_DATA_PENDING` because the INDEC national CPI file was then absent; it estimated no result. Re-running that old ID now would not create prospective history.
- **New independent measurement:** fixed `data/vintages/indec_ipc/all@2026-10-09T121609Z.parquet`, with INDEC national CPI *core* as primary and headline/services as labelled secondary checks. The new primary is a different CPI component and a time window wholly after the old primary endpoint: **2025-09 through 2026-08**. Manifest: `data/manifests/fetch_run_2026-10-09T121824Z.yaml`; SHA-256 `f093e71a89254c3c51d265bc45f1b4ef58e7e59a47113dd4e59416620b03bd98`; source is Argentina's datos.gob.ar/INDEC national IPC series, with monthly rate derived from the pinned index. The separate official headline-index file is `data/vintages/indec/148.3_INIVELNAL_DICI_M_26@2026-10-09T121947Z.parquet`, manifest `fetch_run_2026-10-09T121949Z.yaml`, SHA-256 `232c9a368289b5886b32bf31bda74b66d8b96a578f63b874167334b0c4d78fc1`.
- **Frozen endpoint proposed before value access:** median core CPI month-on-month percent change across the 12 months, plus the count of months at or below 3.0%. A precise directional rule would call broad durability supported only if median core inflation is at most 3.0% **and** at least 9/12 months are at most 3.0%; refuted if median exceeds 3.0%; otherwise mixed. Report headline and services trajectories without changing the primary rule. This uses the old stated 3% inflation benchmark on a new core component and later period; it does not retest the old t+10/t+20 claim.
- **Minimum complete units:** one national monthly series with all 12 consecutive months (12 core observations); the pinned file has 12/12 non-null core month-on-month records in this window, and 12/12 for each secondary component. A single country supports a time-series description, not a policy-causal effect or a comparison against Latin American stabilisations. No 2026 IMF projection is involved.
- **Steelman to register:** persistence can reflect monetary policy, exchange rates, recession, index composition, or global prices as well as the post-2023 reform package. A failed core endpoint would show that headline disinflation did not broaden; a passing endpoint would establish only observed durability.

### 2. Spain: 2024–25 national-accounts and labour trajectory — design-ready as an out-of-time peer check

- **Old ID:** `spain_sanchez_economic_trajectory_2018_2023` (queue rank 283). The old sample ends in **2023**. Its run used a WDI GDP-per-capita panel (171 observations, nine countries, 2005–2023) and reported `PARTIAL`; house-price-to-income and real-wage outcomes remained missing. This follow-up must narrow the claim to output and employment and must not repair the old mixed verdict by relabelling it.
- **Fixed new sample:** 2024 and 2025 only, Spain plus exactly seven euro-area peers **FRA, ITA, PRT, GRC, DEU, NLD, BEL** (Eurostat codes ES, FR, IT, PT, EL, DE, NL, BE). Ireland is omitted from the fixed peer set because the old design itself flagged its GDP distortion. All four selected data series have complete 2024 and 2025 cells for all eight countries: 16 country-years per series. No old 2018–2023 outcome observation enters the endpoint.
- **Direct non-WDI sources:** Eurostat `nama_10_gdp` (`B1GQ`, `CLV20_MEUR`) divided by `demo_pjan` (`NR`, `TOTAL`, `T`) for real output per resident; `lfsi_emp_a` (`EMP_LFS`, `Y15-64`, `T`, `PC_POP`) for employment rate; `une_rt_a` (`Y15-74`, `T`, `PC_ACT`) as a secondary unemployment cross-check. Exact vintages and SHA-256 are listed below. The population denominator is the pinned 1-January estimate, a known annualisation approximation fixed equally for all countries.
- **Frozen endpoint proposed before value access:** (a) Spain's log change in real output per resident from 2024 to 2025 minus the median of the seven peer log changes; and (b) Spain's 2024-to-2025 employment-rate change in percentage points minus the median peer change. A clear two-endpoint rule: supported descriptive resilience if **both** differences are at least zero; refuted descriptive resilience if **both** are negative; mixed otherwise. Unemployment is secondary, without changing the two-endpoint rule. This is a single out-of-time change, not a two-way FE estimate of the 2018 government effect.
- **Minimum complete units:** one Spain plus seven prespecified peers, each observed in both years (16 country-years for each source); all eight are present. Any loss of one country or year voids this exact peer-median design pending a newly registered rule, rather than silently changing the donor pool.
- **Steelman to register:** common euro shocks are partly controlled by contemporaneous peers, but tourism, migration, labour reform, country-specific cycles, and policy changes since 2023 can explain Spain's trajectory. Passing these two macro endpoints would not settle housing affordability, real wages, or causal credit to a government.

### 3. Germany: 2025 price/output co-movement after the final closures — descriptive redesign only; causal attribution blocked

- **Old IDs:** `germany_decline_2018_2025_regulatory_not_fiscal` (rank 64) and `nuclear_phaseout_grid_reliability_cost_tradeoff` (rank 155) are related background, **not two independent validations**. The first old run evaluated WDI industrial GVA only through 2022 although its spec window was 2018–2025; it did not resolve the regulatory-versus-fiscal decomposition. The second old run covered the 2005–2024 phase-out cohorts only with a fossil-share surrogate and left industrial prices, volatility, and adequacy data-gated. The local movement record `movements/germany_merkel_nuclear_phaseout_2011.yaml` records the final reactor closures on 2023-04-15.
- **Revised, directly measured endpoint:** compare Germany with a fixed **10-country EU peer set** (FRA, NLD, BEL, ITA, ESP, POL, CZE, AUT, SWE, FIN). For real manufacturing GVA use Eurostat `nama_10_a10` (`nace_r2=C`, `na_item=B1G`, `unit=CLV_I20`) in 2024 and 2025. For medium industrial electricity price use Eurostat `nrg_pc_205` (`siec=E7000`, `nrg_cons=MWH500-1999`, `unit=KWH`, `currency=EUR`, `tax=X_VAT`) in 2024-S1/S2 and 2025-S1/S2. Both panels have full selected cells for DE and all ten peers: **22 country-years of manufacturing** and **44 country-semesters of price**. The price is a fixed Eurostat band/definition, rather than the old unobserved IEA price target.
- **Frozen endpoint proposed before value access:** Germany's 2024-to-2025 log real manufacturing-GVA growth minus the median peer growth, paired with Germany's 2024-to-2025 log change in the two-semester mean medium-industry electricity price minus the median peer price change. A negative manufacturing gap together with a positive price gap would support only a measured **co-movement** description; opposite signs would cut against that description; other combinations are mixed. Treat the two as one joint descriptive test, with no inference from separate cherry-picked legs.
- **Minimum complete units:** DE plus all ten named peers, each with both annual GVA cells and all four semiannual price cells. This source/policy contrast is new, but 2024–25 falls within the first old spec's *planned* window; its independence is from the old **run and source**, not from every old intended calendar year. It cannot be marketed as a clean independent replication of the old claim.
- **Causal gate:** **blocked**. Germany's final closures coincide with the post-2022 gas/war shock and other energy and industrial changes. The available SMARD series is Germany-only wholesale price/generation (not a matched national industrial-price comparator or adequacy outcome). No pinned design here separates reactor loss from gas price, gas dependence, demand, taxes, or sector mix. Even concordant 2025 price and manufacturing gaps cannot identify the reactor closure effect, the old full price-volatility-LOLE composite, or the regulatory-versus-fiscal channel share.
