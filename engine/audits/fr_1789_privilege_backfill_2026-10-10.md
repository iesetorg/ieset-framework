# French 1789 provincial-privilege step — source review, 2026-10-10

## Decision

One additional pre-1804 signed step is defensible as a **weak, formal legal-content coding**: `institutional.rule_of_law + weak` for FRA in **1789**, tied specifically to the August Decrees' Article 10 removal of particular provincial and municipal privileges. The signed year is **1789 because the royal letters patent conferring legal force were published on 3 November**, not because the Assembly opened debate or first voted on 4 August. This is not an observed rule-of-law index or evidence of equal enforcement.

The [French National Assembly history](https://www.assemblee-nationale.fr/dyn/histoire-et-patrimoine/revolution-francaise/nuit-du-4-aout-abolition-des-privileges) says decrees were adopted 4–11 August, needed royal letters patent to have force of law, and that the letters were published 3 November 1789. It separately states that some feudal dues remained redeemable and full abolition came only in 1792–1793. A [Conseil d'État decision published by Légifrance](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/ceta/id/CETATEXT000007614920) quotes Article 10: particular privileges of provinces, principalities, towns, and communities, pecuniary or otherwise, were abolished without return and brought under the common law of French people; the court applied that provision to reject a claimed historic provincial tax exception. This supports a durable, narrow national legal-equality change. It does not establish equality for women, enslaved people, colonial subjects, or all local practice.

The [current axis definition](../../axes.yaml) includes equal treatment before law and gives `+` the meaning stronger rule of law. `weak` reflects Article 10's limited subject and the gap between promulgation and lived enforcement. The archived `fr_august_decrees_1789` child used an inaccurate 4 August `enacted_date` and `+ strong` coding for the whole feudal order. The candidate preserves that child ID but rewrites it to the limited Article 10 claim, records `effective_date: 1789-11-03`, and links it reciprocally to a focused one-policy movement `fr_constituent_provincial_privilege_abolition_1789`. The archived broader `fr_constituent_assembly_legal_reordering_1789_1791` movement is not imported. The Declaration of Rights and archived property-rights score remain withheld; this step does not count either one.

## U.S. constitutional candidate

The active `us_constitutional_government_1789` child remains policy-only. The [National Archives timeline](https://www.archives.gov/founding-docs/constitution-q-and-a) distinguishes legal government start on 4 March 1789 from the congressional quorum on 6 April, executive operation on 30 April, and Supreme Court organization on 2 February 1790. The [Constitution](https://www.archives.gov/founding-docs/constitution-transcript) creates a Supreme Court, judicial tenure protection, and federal supremacy, but the archived Federalist movement's `+ moderate` rule-of-law claim attributes those institutions and the later Bill of Rights to 4 March. Its market-competition and spending axes also do not follow from the provisions. Its `financial_deregulation -` contradicts the current axis sign.

The [Judiciary Act signed 24 September 1789](https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/federal-judiciary-act) is a distinct, source-backed federal-court policy with narrower jurisdiction than Article III allowed and a durable institutional structure. A separate child and movement could be reviewed for `institutional.rule_of_law + weak` at **24 September 1789**, limited to formal federal adjudication; it should not be assigned to the 4 March constitutional commencement marker or presented as equal treatment throughout state and territorial jurisdictions. This candidate does not add any U.S. signed axis or a token neutral movement.

## Display follow-up

With `year_min` now 1789, that rule hides all five unscored 1789–1844 cards, including the U.S. constitutional commencement. A display change should select policy-only records before the first composite step (currently 1846), keep their dashed markers, and describe them as unscored policy context even when their dates fall inside the axis-only drift grid. It must not label the U.S. 1789 policy as scored.
