Overview and Citation
This codebook describes the variables contained in the Chronology of Constitutional
Events, a comprehensive listing of the year of constitutional adoption, replacement,
and amendment for all independent states since 1789. The data are contained in
ccpcce_v6.txt, a tab-delimited text file (also available in CSV, Stata, and Excel formats),
and have been analyzed in Elkins, Ginsburg, and Melton (2009), and serve as the
baseline census for most of the data collected as part of the Comparative Constitutions
Project (CCP). Notably, this dataset on the chronology of constitutional events
complements the CCP's core dataset on the characteristics of national constitutions,
which is released in a separate file with separate documentation. Please cite the
chronology data as follows:

           Elkins, Zachary, Tom Ginsburg, and James Melton. 2026. "Chronology of

           Constitutional Events, Version 6." Comparative Constitutions Project. Last

           modified: April 6, 2026 [first edition published 2009]. Available at:

           http://www.comparativeconstitutionsproject.org.


Concepts
The chronology is described in detail on the Comparative Constitutions Project's
website and in Elkins, Ginsburg, and Melton (2009); this document provides summary
information for users. The authors utilize two concepts to categorize constitutional
texts. A constitutional system encompasses the period in which a constitution is in
force, a period bracketed by the year of adoption and that of any replacement or
suspension. A constitutional event is any change to a country's constitution, including
adoption, amendment, suspension, or reinstatement. Therefore, within constitutional
systems, there can be (and often are) multiple events. The United States, for example,
has had two constitutional systems: 1781-89, and 1789-present. The present US
constitutional system has included multiple events, including one for each year in
which an amendment is promulgated.

Sample
The unit of analysis is the country-year. Thus, when coding the characteristics of
constitutions, for years in which there are multiple events, the authors code the
content of the constitution as it stood in force at the end of the year. For example, if a
constitution was amended the same year as it was adopted, the authors will code the
content of the constitution in its amended form rather than as originally adopted. The
chronology file, however, identifies the event type of such years with the event that
occurred first chronologically (most commonly a 'new' event).

The sample included in this release of the chronology consists of all years from 1789 to
2025 for all independent countries. The year refers to the year the constitutional event
was promulgated. The list of independent countries is based on that of Ward and
Gleditsch (2020 [1999]) and, in some cases, is supplemented by the Correlates of War
project's "State System Membership List." This sample includes 20,638 country-years,
from which we identified constitutional systems and constitutional events. As of 2025,
every independent state in the world had a constitution in force by our definition,
though as discussed in Elkins, Ginsburg, and Melton (2009) some countries, including
Israel and Saudi Arabia, have unconventional forms. Notably, there are a number of
countries in our sample that had a constitution in place when they achieved
independence; these cases also have system numbers, even though the start date
precedes the observation time.

Errors and Omissions
The writing and promulgation of formal constitutions frequently occurs during periods
of crisis and the historical record of events is not always clear. The authors continually
update the event data, as information emerges. We kindly ask users of these data to
help improve the data's reliability by reporting any mistakes and/or differences in
interpretation to us via the contact form on the CCP website
(http://www.comparativeconstitutionsproject.org/contact).
