# Steelman: argentina_milei_disinflation_monetary_reconfiguration_2023_2028

## Counter-argument

Argentina's disinflation to the ~30 percent plateau in 2025-26 is exactly
where every previous stabilization stalled: the crawl converts into
inertia, the fiscal anchor depends on windfall export-tax revenue and
discretionary adjustment programs, and the FX gap closed only because the
cepo was relaxed at a managed rate — a species of the 2016-2018 gradualism
that ended in the 2018 run. Monetary base growth collapsed partly because
high real rates (LELIQ at deeply positive real yields) crowded the base
out — a stock composition shift, not proof of discipline durability.

## Strongest empirical objections

1. **Plateau stall**: y/y CPI stuck near 30-34 percent through 2025-26 is
   consistent with a new inertial equilibrium, not a completed
   reconfiguration; the "half from peak" leg clears on arithmetic while
   monthly inflation remains 3x the top of any credible target band.

2. **Reserves composition**: the reserves rebound includes swap lines and
   IMF-program disbursements, not purely market accumulation; the level
   metric can clear while quality of reserves deteriorates.

3. **Reversal base rate**: both prior stabilizations (1991, 2016) cleared
   early-window metrics similar to these before reversing; a 2023-2026
   window cannot distinguish durability from the two-year mark of prior
   failures.

## What would change our mind

Monthly CPI failing to break 2 percent m/m through 2027, the FX gap
reopening above 10 percent on cepo re-tightening, or base money growth
re-accelerating above 40 percent as LELIQ stock rolls off would each mark
the reconfiguration as another stalled stabilization.
