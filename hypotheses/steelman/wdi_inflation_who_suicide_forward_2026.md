# Steelman: earlier inflation volatility and later suicide mortality

Unstable consumer prices can strain household planning and economic security.
Countries with more volatile CPI inflation during 2005–2013 might later show
a larger rise in the all-age, both-sex suicide mortality rate from 2014 to
2021. A fixed inverse-hyperbolic-sine transform of the nine annual inflation
rates' sample standard deviation limits leverage from high-inflation regimes.
The model controls the 2014 suicide rate and the transformed mean earlier
inflation rate.

This pairing is especially vulnerable to ecological inference. CPI inflation
is not individual hardship, and WHO suicide-rate estimates depend on
registration quality, cause-of-death coding, and modelling. Mental-health
care, conflict, demographics, macroeconomic crises, and the 2020–2021
pandemic are not identified away by temporal ordering or two controls.
There is no person-level exposure or mental-health treatment here.

A fixed sample, material-volatility, influence, and leave-one-out design
can honestly yield INCONCLUSIVE, PARTIAL, or an opposite-sign result.
Even a strong association would be descriptive only and give zero causal,
policy, school, position, or featured credit.
