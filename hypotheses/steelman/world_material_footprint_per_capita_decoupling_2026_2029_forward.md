# World income and material footprint per person, 2026–2029

## Strongest case

The degrowth critique holds that consumption-based raw-material demand remains tied to global income even when production becomes more efficient and some countries move material-intensive production abroad. On the future 2026–2029 endpoints, a substantial real-income gain may therefore occur without a 5% fall in world material footprint per person. A world aggregate avoids counting a rich-country fall that merely shifts extraction elsewhere.

## Strongest alternative

Material efficiency, substitution, circular use, longer-lived products, and changes in consumption could lower raw-material equivalents while real income rises. A 5% fall over three years would be evidence of per-capita absolute decoupling in this specific window. It would not settle whether total world extraction fell, whether ecological impacts fell, or whether the pace is sufficient for planetary boundaries. Conversely, a flat footprint does not show that degrowth policies would improve welfare.

## Evidence boundary

This is one descriptive forecast using future OWID/UNEP World material-footprint-per-capita and World Bank WDI real GDP-per-capita endpoints. The material series is model-derived and currently ends in 2022, so its revisions and scope require source review. No run or school score exists at registration. Official measurement references: [OWID material footprint per capita](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/material-footprint-per-capita), [UN SDG 12.2.1 metadata](https://unstats.un.org/sdgs/metadata/files/Metadata-12-02-01.pdf), and [World Bank GDP per capita](https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/NY.GDP.PCAP.KD).
