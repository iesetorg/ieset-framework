# Steelman: unemployment rate after inflation acceleration

## Claim and evidence boundary

Across the fixed 187-economy structural panel, a larger three-year increase in bounded IMF annual CPI inflation predicts a higher subsequent three-year change in the modeled unemployment rate. This is a retrospective, descriptive country-year prediction, not a policy intervention or causal estimate.

## Strongest case

A persistent inflation acceleration may forecast monetary tightening, real-income pressure, and later joblessness. A three-year horizon allows the labor market to adjust rather than treating inflation and unemployment as same-year opposites.

## Strongest alternative

A demand expansion can raise inflation while lowering unemployment; supply shocks can raise both. The modeled unemployment series is not a direct administrative count and may change method or coverage. Neither sign by itself proves a Phillips curve or policy effect.
