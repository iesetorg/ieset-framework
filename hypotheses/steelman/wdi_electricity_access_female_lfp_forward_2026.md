# Electricity access and later female labour-force participation

Reliable electricity can free time from household production, power workplaces, extend usable hours, and support sectors that employ women. A decade of earlier coverage growth could be associated with subsequent labour-market participation gains. The exact forward windows avoid using later access to explain earlier participation.

The strongest challenge is that access coverage need not deliver reliable, affordable power. Development, fertility, education, urbanization, social norms, migration, and sector change can jointly shift access and women’s labour supply. Female LFP includes unemployed women and excludes unpaid domestic work; the WDI series is an ILO-modelled estimate. Baseline controls and country-level timing do not isolate an electricity effect, let alone a policy effect.
