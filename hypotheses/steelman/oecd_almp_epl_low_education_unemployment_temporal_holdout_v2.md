# Steelman: OECD ALMP × EPL COVID-period calibration

The strongest interpretation of the claim is a narrow moderation pattern: countries with stricter 2019 employment protection might get less immediate unemployment relief from an increase in active labour-market spending. Pre-existing rules could slow reallocation or blunt the effect of activation programmes. A two-year, next-year comparison could reveal whether that *measured association* persists outside the old 1996–2019 sample.

The strongest rival account is that 2020–2022 was an exceptional macroeconomic and measurement regime. ALMP budgets rose in response to weak labour markets, GDP share denominators moved sharply, job-retention schemes kept many workers classified as employed, and low-education workers faced unusually unequal shutdown and reopening patterns. A spending/EPL interaction could therefore proxy the pandemic shock or different labour-force definitions. The [OECD's COVID labour-market review](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/oecd-employment-outlook-2021_5a700c4b-en/full-report/labour-market-developments-the-unfolding-covid-19-crisis_05296554.html) says unemployment alone missed substantial labour slack; its [education review](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/education-at-a-glance-2021_b35a14e5-en/full-report/component-10.html) notes changes in inactivity among low-educated adults. OECD [SOCX](https://www.oecd.org/en/data/datasets/social-expenditure-database-socx.html) reports programme spending but not exogenous assignment, while its [EPL indicators](https://www.oecd.org/en/data/datasets/oecd-indicators-of-employment-protection.html) summarize legal protection rather than all complementary institutions.

The frozen test requires both COVID exposure years for each country, an exact one-year outcome lag, 2019 EPL moderation, explicit source dimensions, a 30-country floor, clustered uncertainty, and a two-sided 0.01 cutoff. A failure of the floor or rank test is inconclusive. A supported or refuted sign only describes this short sample. It does not establish the Danish or German reform mechanism, a policy effect, or school-score credit.
