# Historical credit booms and crisis onsets: strongest interpretation

The original Jordà-Schularick-Taylor historical panel makes the mechanism
testable over a century that the archived post-1980 BIS/OWID screen could not
cover. Credit to the non-financial private sector and nominal GDP are measured
in the same local currency; their ratio captures the scale of lending relative
to the economy. An unusually large five-year increase can signal a buildup of
fragile balance sheets. A systemic crisis starting during the following five
years is the observable prediction.

The strongest competing interpretation is that both credit booms and banking
crises follow other historical forces: global capital flows, fixed exchange
rates, industrialisation, financial liberalisation, and common downturns.
Archival splices may change measurement across time. The single pooled risk
ratio does not distinguish these pathways or assign a regulatory cause.

The design freezes thirteen nonoverlapping forecast windows per country,
excludes windows touching the two world wars, and calibrates a top quintile
within each of three eras before reading crisis outcomes. JST codes 1 only
in the first year of each crisis. An at-risk window has no coded onset in
the preceding five years. The historical claim would be weakened if the
high-credit group has no elevated forward onset risk, and supported only by
a risk ratio at least two with a minimum absolute difference and event floors.
Missing or thin evidence yields INCONCLUSIVE. This is descriptive evidence,
with no causal, policy, or school-position credit.
