# Steelman: RTA partner expansion and exported HS6 product count

A larger agreement-covered market may let exporters cover the fixed costs of
bringing more product categories to foreign buyers. A count of exported HS6
categories is a direct, if coarse, way to look for this extensive-margin
response. Conditioning on the pre-event count level and trend asks whether an
expansion year predicts an additional forward change rather than merely
continuing an established product-count trajectory.

The strongest countercase is selection. Countries may expand agreements when
export variety is already changing for reasons the panel cannot measure.
The CEPII derived flag records a net increase in partners with RTA status;
it does not establish an agreement's tariff schedule, enforcement, depth,
or effective access for a particular exporter. Prior RTA coverage can remain
in both event and comparison windows. WITS HS6 counts can change with
reporting coverage or nomenclature and do not encode sophistication,
values, or domestic product choice. Country and year effects plus measured
prior trend do not remove these problems.

The proposed result therefore calibrates a conditional association only.
A null, opposite-signed, or inconclusive result is informative and remains
visible. No school or policy score should use it.
