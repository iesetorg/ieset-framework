# age-standardized current tobacco-smoking prevalence decline and later premature NCD mortality

A decline in age-standardized current tobacco-smoking prevalence decline could reduce later exposure-related disease burdens and deaths. The fixed 2000–2010 exposure window precedes the 2010–2021 mortality window, leaving a plausible delay. The four-disease WHO outcome is an interpretable broad health measure.

The strongest challenge is that countries differ in health care, income, diagnosis, population histories, mortality registration, and multiple prevention policies. Baseline levels and WHO-region adjustment do not remove those causes. The smoking series is modelled, while PM2.5 is an estimated ambient concentration. WHO warns that its 2021 NCD mortality estimates can miss indirect pandemic deaths in weak-registration settings. An age-standardized 15+ smoking rate or country total PM2.5 category is not the lifetime exposure of the 30–70 mortality cohort.

The claim is therefore only a cross-country descriptive prediction. A failed frozen gate remains INCONCLUSIVE; no alternative country subset or lag is chosen after the result. No policy, causal, school, position, or featured credit follows from a directional verdict.
