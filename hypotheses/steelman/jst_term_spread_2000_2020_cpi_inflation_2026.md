# Steelman: jst_term_spread_2000_2020_cpi_inflation_2026

The strongest objection is that the anchor-year yield curve is endogenous. Long rates encode expected inflation and risk premia, while short rates respond to current conditions. Long yields may embed expected inflation, so a positive association can be a forecast rather than a causal price effect. A strong predictive slope could therefore arise without a monetary-policy mechanism.

The three eras are disjoint in calendar time, but all twelve tests share one publisher, an 18-country roster, and correlated macroeconomic shocks. This family supplies neither external replication nor identification of a policy effect. The fixed country-and-anchor specification omits time-varying confounders by design, and a weak within-effect spread or country concentration must make the result inconclusive rather than invite a new sample.

To survive this objection, the cpi inflation slope must clear its frozen magnitude and family-adjusted significance thresholds and retain the predicted sign under every country deletion. Even then the conclusion is a narrow predictive association in 2000_2020, with no causal or school credit.
