# Steelman — earlier health spending and later service coverage

Health systems need staff, medicines, facilities, and recurring service
capacity. A rise in total current health expenditure relative to GDP during
2000–2010 could precede better coverage of essential services during
2010–2020, especially where 2010 coverage was low. The WHO service coverage
index contains selected tracer measures for maternal and child health,
infectious disease, noncommunicable disease, and service capacity. A positive
forward association would be compatible with that capacity channel.

The sharp counterargument is confounding. Spending as a GDP share can rise
when GDP falls; it includes household and private payments as well as public
outlays. Higher prior spending may respond to poor coverage or disease
burden. Income, institutions, age structure, urbanisation, and changes in
tracer measurement can move both variables. The WHO composite includes
modelled proxies and cannot establish quality of care, mortality,
affordability, or financial protection. The 2020 endpoint also crosses the
pandemic. Conditioning on initial coverage and the earlier coverage trend
limits a simple catch-up explanation but does not identify a causal effect.

A null or negative result under the frozen rule should be published as such.
Neither direction licenses a public-spending intervention claim. The old
generic public-health-spending/avoidable-mortality REFUTED verdict remains a
separate archive result.
