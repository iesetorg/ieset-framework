# Steelman: Negative affect balance after household debt-service change

A higher household debt-service ratio commits more household income to principal and interest. That squeeze could later raise the adverse-oriented OECD measure: negative affect balance. The predeclared timing, exact country-year roster and four-test correction give this narrow pattern a genuine opportunity to fail.

The strongest alternative is that Gallup World Poll country estimates are survey based, may pool three years, and often sample about 1,000 respondents annually; adjacent changes can overlap mechanically. Rising DSR can also reflect higher incomes, changing rates, housing booms or borrower composition. The aggregate country series never identifies whether the people represented by the outcome have debts. A reverse sign, weak association, failed sample gate, or fragile inference must remain visible. Neither a positive association nor a null establishes a policy effect, welfare mechanism, or school-level claim.
