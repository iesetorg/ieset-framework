# Steelman: female participation and later income growth

## Strongest case

An increase in women's labour-force participation can raise employed labour per person, household resources, and aggregate output. A lagged income-growth endpoint is a harder prediction than a same-year correlation.

## Strongest alternative

Growth, education, fertility, care provision, and structural change can all move participation and later income. Rising participation can also be a response to hardship. The 2005 participation level and prior growth controls address some baseline differences but cannot identify an effect.

## Falsifiers and limits

A significant opposite-signed forward coefficient refutes this narrow direction. Insufficient variation, invalid endpoints, or a concentrated estimate is inconclusive. An estimable small or imprecise coefficient is partial. The original panel's gender-gap magnitude and income-group heterogeneity remain untested here.
