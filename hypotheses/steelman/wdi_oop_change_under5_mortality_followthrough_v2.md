# Steelman: out-of-pocket financing and later child mortality

An out-of-pocket share is closer to the proposed financing burden than
total health expenditure divided by GDP. A long lag also avoids treating
the same year's mortality as an instantaneous response to spending. If a
rising household-payment share constrains access, later under-five
mortality could improve more slowly even after prior mortality and income
trends are held fixed.

The strongest alternative is reverse and common causation. Household
payments can rise because incomes, private care, disease burden, public
budgets, and insurance coverage change. The WDI under-five series is
modelled, and the 2015–2023 interval spans COVID-19 and conflict. Current
health expenditure as a GDP share is an incomplete measure of real service
capacity. Missingness can select the sample. A statistically clear slope
would therefore remain descriptive and cannot credit any fiscal policy or
the old claims about avoidable mortality or medical impoverishment.
