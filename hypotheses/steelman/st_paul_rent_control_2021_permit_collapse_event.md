# Steelman: st_paul_rent_control_2021_permit_collapse_event

## Counter-argument

The 2022 national permit collapse was driven by the fastest mortgage-rate
hiking cycle in four decades and pandemic-era construction-cost inflation;
St Paul's decline coincided with both. Minneapolis's zoning reforms
(2040 plan) shifted its composition toward multifamily in the same window,
making it a non-parallel control rather than a clean one, and the September
2022 amendment may reflect developer expectations that had already adjusted.

## Strongest empirical objections

1. **Rate cycle confound**: permit declines of comparable magnitude
   occurred in uncontrolled Midwest metros in 2022-2023; the synthetic
   donor pool must absorb the rate path, not just level differences.

2. **Composition vs count**: developers may have re-timed filings around
   the amendment rather than cancelled projects, so unit counts recovered
   by 2024 even if annual filings fell.

3. **Anticipation effects**: the ordinance was on the November 2021 ballot
   from mid-2021; some pull-forward and pause happened before the effective
   date, compressing the measured event window.

## What would change our mind

If the St Paul-minus-synthetic gap is small relative to the 2022 national
decline, or if the amendment-reversal leg shows no recovery while
Minneapolis' composition shift explains its stability, the supply-response
claim loses its flagship US case and the axis prior should revert toward
"caps without exemptions bind mainly at the margin of new construction."
