# Steelman: wits_tariff_decline_exported_hs6_breadth_forward_2026

A lower import-weighted applied tariff can expose firms to more inputs, competition, and export-market opportunities. It could precede broader exported HS6 coverage or less concentrated destination/product sales. This is a measurable *association*, not an assigned policy shock.

The strongest rival account is joint development and import-weight changes: richer or faster-growing economies may lower their weighted tariff average and diversify exports for reasons outside tariff policy. Commodity prices, exchange rates, trade agreements, classification revisions, reporting gaps, and composition shifts also matter. Prior export trend, baseline export structure, income controls, reporter effects, and year effects reduce simple confounding but cannot identify an effect.

A negative or null coefficient is a substantive calibration against the directional claim. The frozen source, sample, practical size (0.01 log1p-count point per tariff percentage point), three-cell family cutoff, and all failures must remain visible. Product and market concentration use distinct measures; the WITS `product_concentration` local series is actually `HH-MKT-CNCNTRTN-NDX`, export-**market** HHI.
