# Steelman and failure modes: government effectiveness and exported HS6 breadth

Reliable customs administration, contract enforcement, trade services, and public infrastructure can help firms keep more product lines in export markets. A rise in the World Bank government-effectiveness estimate could therefore precede an increase in the number of HS6 products a reporter exports to the world. This study freezes one forward descriptive association; it does not isolate any of those channels.

The strongest rival explanation is common development. Rising income, private investment, transport capacity, exchange rates, trade agreements, and export demand may coincide with a higher WGI estimate and broader reported exports. The fixed GDP controls, prior export-breadth level and trend, reporter effects, and year effects only partly address those factors. Prior export breadth may itself affect perceptions of effectiveness; controlling for its change over the treatment window can also block part of a genuine pathway. WGI estimates pool changing perception and administrative inputs, and overlapping three-year windows do not create independent interventions.

WITS counts exported HS6 categories. It does not measure the value, complexity, domestic content, profitability, survival, or welfare of exports. A data collection or HS-classification break can change the count. This pinned WITS Parquet has no observation-status or method-break field, so no normal-status claim is possible. After the separate source lock, a nonfinite, negative, or fractional selected count is excluded under the fixed rule; source-level missingness may make the sample too small and yield INCONCLUSIVE. No alternate endpoint or year range replaces it.

A sufficiently large positive coefficient would support this narrow forward association. A sufficiently large negative coefficient would refute its direction. Otherwise an estimable result is PARTIAL. None of these outcomes validates a policy, the previous CEPII-to-WITS study, a school prediction, or a causal effect.
