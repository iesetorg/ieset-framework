# Steelman: Upper-secondary attainment, adults 25+ after electricity-access gain

The strongest descriptive case is that household and community electrification can make evening study, school administration, communication, and teachers’ work easier. National electricity access is broad enough to reveal a common development margin, and a five-year earlier gain is temporally ordered before the three-year change in upper-secondary attainment, adults 25+. A positive association can be useful for describing where these transitions co-occur.

Adult upper-secondary attainment is a cohort stock. A short follow-up may largely capture cohort replacement rather than the effect of recent household electricity. The national access measure does not identify actual school electricity, reliability, price, or who gains access. Rising income, urbanization, health, migration, institutional quality, education policy, survey changes, and trends can drive both variables. Country/year effects and baseline controls cannot isolate a policy intervention. Other four milestones in the family share a source and mechanism; five positive coefficients would not be five independent replications. This case earns no causal, policy, school-score, position, or featured credit.
