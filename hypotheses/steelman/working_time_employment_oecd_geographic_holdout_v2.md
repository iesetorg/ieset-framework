# Working time and employment: country holdout

Shorter average working time might coincide with stable employment because firms
reallocate work across employees, output per hour rises, or services and part-
time jobs expand. The measured claim is modest: across OECD countries omitted
from the earlier panel, a change in annual hours per worker is associated with
less than half a proportional change in the employment-to-population ratio.

The strongest objection is endogeneity. Employment growth can change the
composition of workers and their average hours, and both can respond to a
recession or labour-market reform. Country and year effects plus income,
population, trade, and service-share controls do not isolate a shorter-hours
policy. A null coefficient with a wide interval would also be uninformative.
The registered equivalence interval and precision gate therefore require
affirmative evidence of a small association. The France and Iceland policy
events remain outside this holdout.

countries, but source vintages can overlap other IESET work. This study earns
