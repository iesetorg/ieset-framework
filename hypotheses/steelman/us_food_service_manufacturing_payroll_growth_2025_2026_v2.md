# Steelman: 2025-26 food-services versus manufacturing payroll growth

The strongest contrary view is that these two industries do not isolate the
old post-COVID labor-reallocation claim. Manufacturing is a new comparator,
not the old professional/information sector, and food services are only one
part of leisure and hospitality. National payroll counts do not measure wage
over-recovery, hours, retail employment, quits, or work-from-home persistence.
An apparent gap can reflect demand, migration, productivity, automation,
industry-specific cycles, or common revisions rather than durable reallocation.

The saved old five-metric run is INCONCLUSIVE_PENDING_DATA. This new-ID test
cannot fill its missing metrics or change that status. Both BLS series are
seasonally adjusted and their August 2025-August 2026 months follow the old
2024 endpoint. The October 2026 vintage was already available when this rule
was fixed, but selected payroll values were not inspected. The full 13-month
key sequence, exact source bytes, and one-percentage-point comparison band
are fixed before any value read.

If manufacturing payroll growth exceeds food-services growth by at least one
percentage point, the narrow follow-up is refuted. A gap inside the band is
mixed. Missing or invalid values are inconclusive. No result is a causal
COVID or policy estimate or earns old-case or school-score credit.
