# Steelman: jst_current_account_deficit_2000_2020_crisis_onset_2026

The strongest objection is that external deficits need not be vulnerability. They may finance productive investment in a growing economy, while a surplus may reflect weak domestic demand. Crisis chronologies are sparse, and banking vulnerability depends on credit quality and institutions, not just external flows. Reverse causality can make a future macro pattern appear to be predicted by the initial deficit.

The country-and-anchor comparison omits changing institutions and external shocks by design. All cells use one stitched historical source, share countries and correlated shocks, and are one multiplicity-corrected family rather than independent replications. The specified completeness, influence, and event-count gates must be honored even if they make this cell inconclusive.

To survive the objection, the crisis onset slope must clear its frozen magnitude and fourteen-test Holm threshold and retain its direction after every country deletion. Even then it establishes only a narrow historical predictive association in 2000_2020, with no causal, policy, or school credit.
