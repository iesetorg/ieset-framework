# Atlanta Fed wage-quartile growth gap: steelman

**Registered lead.** If lower-paid continuously employed workers catch up in
nominal hourly pay, the Atlanta Fed median individual 12-month wage-growth
series for the lowest *average-wage-level* quartile should exceed the highest
quartile's series over the fixed January 2024–September 2026 window. A mean
gap beyond +0.5 percentage point with positive means in each calendar year
would be a material, persistent descriptive pattern. The positive direction
was chosen from this contingent theory before either selected group's
magnitudes were examined.

**Best rival account.** Tight labour markets, statutory wage floors, sector
mix, changes in who remains employed, and regression or selection effects can
all alter quartile-specific median growth. Quartiles use the individual's
average wage level across two observations, not a fixed population cohort or
growth-rate ranking. The tracker excludes self-employed workers and people
without a valid 12-month wage match. It measures nominal individual growth,
not changes in the low/high wage-level ratio, real purchasing power, or the
distribution of all workers. A positive gap cannot identify any policy cause.

**Measurement challenge.** The underlying CPS respondents are matched 12
months apart, and the published series is a three-month moving average. The
32 selected months are highly dependent, so the preregistered 12-lag HAC
tail test is a limited time-series screen, not worker-level survey inference.
October 2025 is unavailable owing to the government shutdown; the publisher
used alternate months for the following moving averages and warned that the
late-2025 CPS sample is smaller. It also reports 2024 topcoding changes and
minor 2023–2025 methodology revisions. A different vintage may revise history.

**Decision boundary.** All 32 fixed monthly pairs must exist and be finite.
Support requires a one-sided HAC(12) result beyond +0.5 percentage point at
p<=0.025 and positive 2024, 2025, and 2026 means. Refutation mirrors the
rule below -0.5 point with three negative year means. Other valid outcomes
are partial. Neither verdict repairs any old hypothesis, earns school-score
credit, or creates a policy-evidence link. The source data were published
before registration, so this is value-blind retrospective calibration.

Source: [Federal Reserve Bank of Atlanta Wage Growth Tracker methodology](https://www.atlantafed.org/research-and-data/data/wage-growth-tracker).
