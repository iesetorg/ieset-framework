# Manufacturing hourly productivity and later high-tech export mix

Rising manufacturing real GVA per hour can signal greater capability to
produce and export research-intensive goods. The proposed outcome is the
three-year change in the high-tech share of manufactured exports, with the
baseline share controlled. This directly tests a production-to-export-mix
association, separate from the earlier resident-patent-to-export-value test.

The strongest objection is measurement and reverse selection. High-tech
export share can rise because lower-tech exports contract, while productivity
growth may reflect hours, demand, exchange rates, or production networks.
The OECD PDB and World Bank export series can share national statistical
inputs. Country and year effects and a three-year lead do not identify a
causal upgrade mechanism or any policy effect. A positive estimate supports
only the registered measured association; a significant negative one refutes
it under the frozen two-test family rule.

This test shares the PDB manufacturing measure and many countries with the
patent-to-productivity test, so the two do not independently replicate a
causal chain. It gets no school, position, policy, or featured credit.
