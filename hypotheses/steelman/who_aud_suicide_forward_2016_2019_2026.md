# Steelman — who_aud_suicide_forward_2016_2019_2026

Heavy or harmful alcohol use can plausibly raise later mortality; the 2016 AUD prevalence measure captures a particularly severe drinking-related condition, and the 2019 endpoint follows it without including pandemic years.

Suicide certification, stigma, and vital-registration completeness differ sharply across countries. AUD prevalence is itself modelled from country traits; rising AUD diagnoses may reflect detection rather than higher true burden. Economic distress, mental health care, means access and changing mortality registration can move suicide without AUD change.

A country association does not show that people with AUD caused the mortality change or that any policy would reverse it. GDP and region controls, baseline history, influence checks, family correction and regional holdouts cannot remove all shared model inputs or confounding. A null, opposing, fragile or non-estimable outcome must be reported.
