# Steelman: nominal depreciation and multi-category HICP inflation

The fixed claim is a **single omnibus** descriptive proposition: among twenty non-nested COICOP groups, at least five show higher annual-average HICP inflation in `t+1` after larger BIS nominal broad EER depreciation at `t`, and none shows a comparably qualified opposite-direction association. Each planned category estimate must individually clear beta ≥ +0.04 HICP percentage points per +1 log-percentage-point depreciation, two-sided `p≤0.01/20`, and every leave-one-country-out beta ≥ +0.02. At least sixteen categories must pass estimation gates. Five qualified negative components with no positive one refute the direction; other estimable patterns are partial. The twenty rows are evidence for one hypothesis, not twenty independent public claims.

A nominal depreciation can raise local-currency prices of imported consumer goods and traded inputs. Cost pass-through can appear with a lag across food, clothing, household equipment, vehicles, household energy, recreation and personal effects. The strongest rival is that these groups differ sharply in import content, regulation and local service content. Excise changes dominate tobacco and alcohol; global oil prices and regulated tariffs affect fuel and energy; domestic wages, retailer inventories, taxes, quality adjustment and pricing-to-market can dominate other baskets. BIS broad EER weights manufacturing trade, not the import structure of each HICP group. Euro-area members share a currency, and country and year fixed effects with two-way clustering do not isolate a currency shock.

The pinned HICP annual Parquet retains no cell flags. The frozen analysis requires finite published rates without asserting break-screened method continuity. HICP annual-average change compares average monthly indices in adjacent years, not December-to-December inflation. Exact source hashes, the key-only roster, all twenty category codes, sample floors, concentration gates, family-wide correction and five-category omnibus rule are fixed before outcome values or nulls are accessed.

IESET already has BIS effective-exchange-rate to headline inflation studies. This omnibus is chosen after wider archive work, not from an independent holdout. One-year ordering does not establish causation or identify a currency policy effect. The result has zero causal, policy, school-score, position and featured credit.
