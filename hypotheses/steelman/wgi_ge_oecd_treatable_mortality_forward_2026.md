# Steelman: government effectiveness and treatable mortality

The favorable mechanism is credible but broad. A government that implements budgets, regulations, procurement, workforce planning, and public services effectively may also maintain timely diagnosis and treatment. A within-country increase in the World Bank Government Effectiveness estimate could therefore precede a lower OECD treatable-mortality rate, even after accounting for real income per person and common annual shocks. The registered outcome is the age- and sex-total OECD TRTM death rate per 100,000, observed at exactly t+1, not overall life expectancy or avoidable mortality from every cause.

The strongest alternative is that the WGI estimate and mortality reflect common, unmeasured changes in health institutions. WGI is an aggregate perception measure, not a randomly assigned intervention or a direct measure of clinical quality. A country may receive a better WGI score partly because observers already perceive its health services to be improving. Conversely, falling mortality may improve perceived service quality. Health expenditure, technology, smoking and alcohol, population age, disease prevalence, migration, and death certification can change together with governance. Income adjustment and country/year effects cannot remove country-specific time-varying confounding. The exact one-year order does not identify an effect, and mortality can respond over longer horizons than one year.

OECD TRTM is a harmonized rate but its country/year cells can differ in cause coding and revisions. The raw file has three observation-status fields; the runner excludes any nonblank status other than A after a separately committed source lock. It does not search for visually favorable series breaks. The complete-case and residual-treatment concentration floors are binding. If coverage or country-cluster inference fails, the result is INCONCLUSIVE even if the fitted sign looks favorable.

This is a new measured source pair, yet older IESET studies already use OECD treatable mortality against health spending and other outcomes against WGI government effectiveness. The pairing is a descriptive calibration of a narrow question, not an independent holdout against the adaptive archive. Every verdict has zero causal, policy, position, school-score, and featured credit. A causal follow-up would need a defensible governance shock, timing and mechanism checks, independent mortality data, and country-specific coding review fixed before inspecting results.
