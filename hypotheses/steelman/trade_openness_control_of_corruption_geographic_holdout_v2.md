# Steelman: trade openness and WGI control-of-corruption estimate holdout

The strongest version of this narrow claim is that cross-border exchange may help countries gain inputs, ideas, and competitive pressure that coincide with a higher WGI control-of-corruption estimate in the following year. This test asks whether the within-country association appears in a country pool completely omitted from the older study, after common calendar shocks, income, and rule of law are accounted for. The old 40-country result was PARTIAL and is disclosed in the new specification.

The strongest rival explanation is: Governance perceptions may jointly influence trade and both WGI series. WGI rule of law and control of corruption overlap in construct and source, so the control can absorb meaningful variation. Changes in a trade-to-GDP ratio can also reflect GDP denominator movements, commodity prices, exchange rates, and global demand. Country and year effects do not resolve these problems.

The frozen endpoint is **wgi:CC.EST**, read as WGI estimate units, at exact calendar t+1. It does not operationalise the old named mechanism. The original 40 countries are excluded; the sample, source hashes, threshold, and nine-test family are fixed before estimation. A favorable result would support a measured association only; an adverse significant result would refute its stated direction, and a weak estimate would remain PARTIAL.
