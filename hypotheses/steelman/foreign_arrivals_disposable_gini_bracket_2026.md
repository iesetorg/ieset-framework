# Steelman: foreign-country arrivals and later disposable-income Gini

An arrival flow could change the distribution of household disposable income
through labour supply, skill composition, household formation, taxes, and
transfers. This two-year bracketing test examines an all-resident Gini change
around the arrival year in a fixed European country panel.

The same flow can follow growth, conflict, or policy changes that also move
inequality. The Eurostat citizenship category includes stateless arrivals and
does not measure foreign-born or net migration. EU-SILC Gini survey years
usually refer to the preceding income year; Ireland's earlier exception is
excluded. The data have no retained observation-status flags. A measured
association cannot isolate migrant-specific or policy effects. A null or
opposite-sign estimate must be retained under the frozen family rule.
