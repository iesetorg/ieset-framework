# Steelman and limits: JST interwar advanced-economy trade share

The strongest reading of this question is narrow: if nominal imports plus exports fell faster than nominal output around the Depression, the trade-to-GDP ratio should fall in the fixed advanced-economy sample. A wide, shared fall across countries would be a credible descriptive fact, even if the exact threshold of eight percentage points is not met. Equal country weights deliberately describe the average sampled economy rather than a world aggregate.

A trade-share drop cannot isolate tariff law from the collapse of income, gold-standard deflation, exchange-rate change, banking distress, and other restrictions. The JST panel omits Argentina and Brazil from the older 14-country specification and is not the world. The selected columns are annual nominal local-currency series; (imports+exports)/GDP is dimensionless within a country, but historical source splices and varying national-account definitions remain material. The pre-registered gate excludes invalid or missing country-years and refuses a result if too few countries or one country dominates.

A supported result therefore means only that the registered advanced-economy trade-to-GDP decline cleared the numerical and breadth gates. It does not demonstrate a Smoot-Hawley effect, a retaliation cascade, a global 65 percent nominal trade loss, or the largest peacetime contraction in the century. A failed or thin source must stay inconclusive; other valid magnitudes can refute or partially fit the threshold. This one question is not multiplied by country, year, import/export leg, or school-position links.
