# Steelman: public ALMP spending and the migrant/native unemployment gap

The strongest version of the claim is that public employment services, training, and hiring support can help unemployed foreign-born workers overcome language, credential, and network barriers that native-born jobseekers face less often. A rise in public active labour market programme spending could therefore precede a narrowing of the foreign-born minus native-born unemployment-rate gap.

The strongest rival explanation is policy response: authorities often increase spending when labour markets worsen, and the GDP denominator of a spending share also rises mechanically in recessions. A negative association might reflect an ordinary recovery or changing migrant composition rather than a programme effect. The SOCX total covers all recipients and does not identify immigrant participation, programme quality, or implementation. The unemployment gap is based on subgroup labour forces and can move if participation changes. OECD immigration and labour surveys may have country-specific breaks that the frozen parquet does not expose as a harmonized quality screen.

This study tests only the measured association of the annual public SOCX TP60 percentage-of-GDP change from t-1 to t with the OECD foreign-born minus native-born unemployment-rate gap change from t to t+1. The primary change-only country/year fixed-effects model, country-cluster uncertainty, two-test Bonferroni p<=0.005 cutoff, and a practical effect threshold are fixed before outcome values. Baseline gap adjustment is a diagnostic ANCOVA-equivalent model; excluding 2022 and limiting t<=2019 are also diagnostic, not alternate opportunities for a verdict. A nonnegative, imprecise, or small association is a substantive failed prediction. No causal programme, policy, school, position, or featured credit can follow.
