# hightech_export_share_income_growth_geographic_holdout_v2: steelman

## Strongest case

A larger high-technology share within manufactured exports can indicate productive capabilities that accompany later income growth. An exact next-year outcome in excluded countries tests whether the measured relationship travels.

## Best contrary case

The export share can rise when low-tech exports contract, and its classification says little about domestic value added or spillovers. Income growth and export mix may share omitted causes. This design cannot establish innovation, diffusion, or welfare effects.

## Measurement boundary

WDI TX.VAL.TECH.MF.ZS is the high-technology share of manufactured exports, not direct innovation, domestic value added, or technology spillovers. This association does not validate the old innovation or welfare labels.

## Frozen decision boundary

Every country in the historical 40-country old specification is excluded. The old PARTIAL result was known before this registration. The fixed 1996-2023 treatment window uses exact t and t+1 observations, finite complete cases, at least five pairs per country, and source files whose SHA-256 values match tracked fetch manifests. A five-test family requires at least 800 pairs and 50 countries, with two-sided p <= 0.02 for either predeclared or opposite significant direction. Other estimable results are PARTIAL; failed sample or estimator gates are INCONCLUSIVE. No result is available at registration. Any eventual result is an associational calibration with zero school or causal credit.
