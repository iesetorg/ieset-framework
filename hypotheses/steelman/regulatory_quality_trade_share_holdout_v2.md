# Steelman: regulatory_quality_trade_share_holdout_v2

Among countries excluded from the old port-competition screen, a higher World Governance Indicators regulatory-quality estimate at year t predicts a higher goods-and-services trade share of GDP at exact year t+1, conditional on real income per person and country and treatment-year effects.

Predictable rules might reduce transaction costs for traders, while geography, country size, commodity mix, and global demand can dominate the trade share.

Regulatory quality is not port competition and the trade-to-GDP ratio does not directly measure transport or border costs. Country and treatment-year effects plus baseline income leave confounding and possible reverse causation. A result in either direction is a measured association, not a policy effect.

The old PARTIAL result was already known; all 40 countries in that historical specification are excluded. The expected sign is fixed by the claim above. Support and refutation use the same two-sided 0.10/11 family threshold after a 400-pair/35-country complete-case gate and five pairs per country. Feasibility selection inspected null masks only, without outcome magnitudes or fits. No school claim receives score credit.
