# Manufacturing productivity and next-year employment — steelman

The directional claim is lower employment growth after higher real output-per-hour growth in manufacturing. A labor-saving mechanism could reduce headcount when a sector can make the same output with fewer hours; expanding output demand and complements can produce the opposite sign. The prospective test therefore records either direction, and a statistically qualified opposite sign refutes this directional claim.

The frozen treatment is OECD PDB GVAHRS GY for ACTIVITY=C, XDC_H/LR. The outcome is the same sector's persons-employed log change from t to t+1. Prior employment growth, country and treatment-year effects are included; this is still descriptive. Sector demand, hours composition, outsourcing, measurement and business cycles can move both measures. The same vintage and country roster are reused in the other two sectors, so all three tests are one correlated family.

A visible result needs the source-lock chronology, complete sample and variation gates, country-cluster inference, Holm correction and hash-fold sign stability. The result cannot support school, position, policy, causal or featured scoring.
