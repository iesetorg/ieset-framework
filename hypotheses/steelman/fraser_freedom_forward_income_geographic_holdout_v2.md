# Fraser economic freedom and next-year income growth: steelman

## Strongest case

A stable legal setting, open exchange, sound money, and predictable rules can make investment and specialization easier. Fraser's aggregate score gives a consistent country-year summary of those conditions. If that channel generalizes, higher scores in countries omitted from the old study should be associated with faster growth in real PPP income per person from t to exact t+1, after fixed country traits, common year shocks, population, trade share, and WGI rule of law are held constant.

## Best contrary case

Higher income may itself support the institutions and data quality that raise Fraser scores. The aggregate has normative weighting, and its components overlap the rule-of-law control. The panel may therefore estimate a small or unstable coefficient even if some institutional features matter. A strong association would still not show that changing an index score causes income growth. This revised growth endpoint does not validate the old income-level claim. The old broader claim that legal-system and sound-money components are the strongest is not the primary verdict here; their separate regressions are descriptive sensitivity checks only.

## Fixed decision boundary

The historical 44-country roster is excluded. Only complete exact t to t+1 pairs in 2000–2021 with at least five years per country enter the primary sample, using the Fraser aggregate, outcome, and three controls. All pinned files must pass hash custody before any fit. After that gate, the legal-system and sound-money analyses each use their available subset of the primary panel, apply the same five-year country screen, and report their own sample sizes; missing component rows cannot remove a primary row or change the verdict. A missing or mismatched pinned component file aborts the run as a source-custody failure. A positive primary aggregate-score coefficient at two-sided country-clustered p ≤ 0.10/3 supports the narrowed association; a negative coefficient at the same threshold refutes it; other estimable results are partial. Coverage below 1,500 pairs or 70 countries, or invalid estimation, is inconclusive. The old partial result was seen and carries no prospective credit.

The local Fraser slices are fixed by SHA-256, but their fetch and derivation manifest was not found. Their upstream acquisition and component transformation are therefore unverified beyond these pinned local bytes. This limits source provenance even if the statistical replay succeeds.

The WDI/WGI source vintages and some eligible countries overlap already fitted IESET v2 tests. This sample is held out from the named old freedom-income run, not from the entire programme. The three-test family rule covers only this new cohort.
