# Steelman — prior safely managed drinking water coverage and later child mortality

The plausible mechanism is that safer safely managed drinking water reduces exposure to enteric infections, and children born or living after expansion face lower mortality. A four-year lead window and controls for mortality baseline, its prior trend, service baseline, income, country and anchor effects make this a more specific descriptive prediction than same-year co-movement.

The strongest alternative is that service coverage rises where mortality decline slows, perhaps due to deliberate investment in places with severe health challenges; a robust positive slope would refute the registered negative direction. Even a negative slope can arise from simultaneous vaccination, nutrition, clinical care, changing fertility or economic growth. WHO/JMP coverage and UN IGME mortality are modeled estimates, so common reporting improvements and smooth trends can also create association.

Water and sanitation exposures are correlated; they form one two-test family. A gate-cleared result is a descriptive calibration only. No result is credited to a policy, school, infrastructure project or causal mechanism.
