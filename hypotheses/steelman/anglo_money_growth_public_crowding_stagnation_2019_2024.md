# Steelman: anglo_money_growth_public_crowding_stagnation_2019_2024

## Counter-argument

The welfare-Anglo stagnation is the product of a terms-of-trade and
rate-shock cycle common to small open economies, not of money growth or
public crowding: Canada's per-capita decline tracks immigration-driven
population accounting; Australia's tracks the China commodity cycle
turning; the UK's tracks Brexit plus the 2022 energy shock; New Zealand's
tracks the end of its pandemic stimulus. Broad money growth of 20-25
percent was near-universal across the OECD including the USA — which
recovered — so money growth cannot be the discriminating variable. The
public-consumption share stayed high partly because GDP denominators were
depressed (mechanical ratio effect), not because transfers ratcheted.

## Strongest empirical objections

1. **USA breaks the mechanism**: comparable money growth with superior
   per-capita and consumption outcomes falsifies a money-side causal
   channel; the pattern, if found, is welfare-state-institutional or
   shock-exposure, not monetary.

2. **Denominator effects**: government-consumption ratios can rise purely
   from weak GDP; share-based ratchet evidence needs real spending levels
   as a robustness leg.

3. **Small-N, short-window**: five units over six years cannot separate
   the policy bundle (fiscal transfers, migration, energy exposure,
   rate path) — the associational label must constrain any scoreboard
   weight.

## What would change our mind

If the high-money-growth welfare-Anglo countries' per-capita growth
converges to the USA by 2026-2027 as denominators normalize, or if public
consumption shares give back more than a point without growth
deteriorating further, the crowding framing loses its descriptive grip
too.
