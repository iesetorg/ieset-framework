# Steelman: private credit income growth holdout v2

Among countries excluded from the old private-credit productivity study, a higher private-sector bank-credit share of GDP at year t predicts faster real GDP-per-person growth from t to exact year t+1, conditional on baseline real income per person and country and treatment-year effects.

Private credit depth can reflect credit booms, reverse causation, and financial development; next-year GDP growth does not establish productivity or welfare. Country and year effects plus baseline income leave omitted causes and reverse causation. A result in either direction is only a measured association.

The old PARTIAL result was known, and all 40 countries in that historical specification are excluded. The new direction is fixed by the claim above. Expected and adverse coefficients face the same five-test two-sided threshold of p<=0.02 after a 400-pair/35-country complete-case gate. No school claim receives score credit.
