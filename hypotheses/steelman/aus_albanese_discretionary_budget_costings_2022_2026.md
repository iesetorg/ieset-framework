# Strongest opposing case

A discretionary decision can cost money and still improve economic management. Health, defence, childcare, infrastructure and recession insurance deliver services and sometimes increase future productive capacity. Tax reductions also worsen a mechanical balance while benefiting taxpayers. This test cannot decide whether those benefits exceed costs.

The actual budget can improve even if new decisions have a negative net costing. Conversely, commodity and nominal-income windfalls do not establish that all policy choices were effective. The comparison must retain both favourable and unfavourable parameter revisions and should not credit either party with an inherited policy sunset.

The main empirical objection is accounting, not rhetoric: overlapping forecast windows, recycled announcements, hidden provisions, changes in accounting classification and unrecorded reversals can produce a fictitious spending total. An independently reconciled fixed-horizon ledger could show small or positive net policy contributions and reject the material claim. A large unexplained residual prevents any verdict.

This design was written after reading headline budget outcomes. Its registration would govern a new reconstruction, not prove that the research question was chosen without knowledge of the period.
