# Steelman: trade openness and hospital beds per 1,000 people holdout

The strongest version of this narrow claim is that cross-border exchange may help countries gain inputs, ideas, and competitive pressure that coincide with greater hospital-bed density in the following year. This test asks whether the within-country association appears in a country pool completely omitted from the older study, after common calendar shocks, income, and rule of law are accounted for. The old 40-country result was PARTIAL and is disclosed in the new specification.

The strongest rival explanation is: Public health budgets and hospital policy determine bed capacity; bed counts do not identify medical-device imports or care quality. Changes in a trade-to-GDP ratio can also reflect GDP denominator movements, commodity prices, exchange rates, and global demand. Country and year effects do not resolve these problems.

The frozen endpoint is **world_bank_wdi:SH.MED.BEDS.ZS**, read as beds per 1,000 population, at exact calendar t+1. It does not operationalise the old named mechanism. The original 40 countries are excluded; the sample, source hashes, threshold, and nine-test family are fixed before estimation. A favorable result would support a measured association only; an adverse significant result would refute its stated direction, and a weak estimate would remain PARTIAL.
