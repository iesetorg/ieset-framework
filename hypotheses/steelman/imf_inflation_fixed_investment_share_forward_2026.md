# Steelman: gross fixed capital formation share after inflation acceleration

## Claim and evidence boundary

Across the fixed 187-economy structural panel, a larger three-year increase in bounded IMF annual CPI inflation predicts a lower subsequent three-year change in gross fixed capital formation as a share of GDP. This is a retrospective, descriptive country-year prediction, not a policy intervention or causal estimate.

## Strongest case

Rapid price acceleration can obscure relative prices and increase financing uncertainty, reducing the fixed-capital spending share in following years. The fixed-capital-formation share measures physical investment intensity, not its productivity.

## Strongest alternative

Inflation may instead coexist with credit expansion, reconstruction, or nominal-growth booms that lift measured investment. External crises, reforms, and public-investment programs can jointly move inflation and capital formation.
