# Steelman: manufacturing unit labour cost and export composition

If manufacturing costs rise relative to output per hour, producers may lose
price competitiveness and manufactured goods may become a smaller share of
merchandise exports over the following three years. The fixed panel asks only
whether that measured association has the predicted sign after country and
year effects and a baseline-share control.

Unit labour costs can reflect exchange rates, wages, productivity, and the
business cycle. A manufactured-export share can fall because commodities rise,
even while manufactured-export volume grows. A negative coefficient would
neither identify a labour-policy effect nor establish a welfare loss. A null
or opposite-sign estimate must be retained under the frozen family rule.
