# Steelman: foreign-citizen arrivals and the migrant/native unemployment gap

The strongest version of the claim is a temporary adjustment effect. A country-year with a larger rise in arrivals of foreign citizens or stateless people per 1,000 residents may add newly arrived jobseekers before employers recognize credentials and language skills, so the foreign-born minus native-born unemployment-rate gap could widen in the following year.

Several rival mechanisms point the other way. Arrivals are selected into countries and years with strong labour demand; many arrive for work; some are not yet in the labour force; some are native-born foreign citizens; naturalized foreign-born workers remain in the OECD outcome. The exposure is a citizenship flow, not a foreign-born stock, net migration, visa change, or immigrant-specific unemployment series. Annual OECD unemployment at t may already reflect arrivals during t. Country/year fixed effects cannot identify an arrival-cohort effect. Baseline gap adjustment is a diagnostic ANCOVA-equivalent model, not the primary estimate.

Eurostat's two age conventions are meaningful for age-specific counts, yet total-age REACH and COMPLET differ for Netherlands 2018 (146,740 versus 76,789 in the pinned vintage); REACH also contains Belgium 2013-14 when COMPLET does not. REACH is the primary selector because the earlier registered Eurostat arrival/Gini study used it and Eurostat defines it as age reached during the year. COMPLET is a mandatory sensitivity. A directional verdict requires both estimates to pass the same sign, family p cutoff, and practical effect threshold. The 2022 exclusion and t<=2019 window disclose sensitivity to the Ukraine period and the pandemic. An unstable, small, or imprecise result remains partial or inconclusive. No causal immigration, labour policy, school, position, or featured credit can follow.
