# Steelman — urban at-least-basic drinking water

The proposed strain mechanism says rapid growth in the urban share can outrun pipes, treatment, networks, or sanitation capacity, slowing later urban urban at-least-basic drinking water coverage. The fixed windows, urban-only WHO measure, baseline service level, prior trend, income controls, and country/anchor effects make this more specific than a national share comparison.

The strongest alternative is that expanding cities make service delivery cheaper and improve coverage; a well-measured positive coefficient would refute the registered negative direction. Migration, reclassification of settlements, government capacity, and jointly improving development outcomes can drive both series. WHO/JMP estimates use regression and extrapolation, while WDI urban shares are smoothed estimates under national definitions; their co-trends may be partly measurement artifacts.

A PARTIAL outcome remains informative only as a public descriptive calibration if all frozen sample and inference gates pass. No observed result is credited to a city policy, infrastructure investment, school, or causal mechanism. The three endpoints are correlated; basic water and safely managed water overlap.
