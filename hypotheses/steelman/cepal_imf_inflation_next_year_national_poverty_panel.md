# Steelman: inflation and next-year national poverty in Latin America

High inflation may erode purchasing power before wages and transfers adjust,
raising measured national poverty in the following year. The fixed panel tests
that direct sign in observed CEPALSTAT poverty shares and IMF annual CPI
inflation, with real GDP growth and country and year effects.

An opposite sign is plausible. Inflation can follow a recovery in nominal
income, and stronger employment or transfer programs can lower poverty while
prices rise. Annual poverty surveys differ in timing and coverage, and their
year labels need not match household exposure to calendar-year inflation.
Country and year effects cannot remove these changing local conditions. IMF
WEO historical data can be revised after national releases. The registered
lag is a temporal convention, not proof of causation.

The test must keep the 14-country roster, the national poverty dimension,
the actual-year WEO cutoff, and the asinh transform fixed. Missing or invalid
cells can make the result inconclusive; they cannot license replacing a
country, year, geography, inflation series, or poverty definition. A positive
estimate with weak precision, an immaterial coefficient, or unstable country
leave-outs is partial evidence, not support. A valid negative material
estimate with the same precision and stability gates refutes this directional
descriptive claim. Any result has zero policy or school-score credit.
