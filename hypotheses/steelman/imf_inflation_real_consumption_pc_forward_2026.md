# Steelman: real household consumption per person after inflation acceleration

## Claim and evidence boundary

Across the fixed 187-economy structural panel, a larger three-year increase in bounded IMF annual CPI inflation predicts lower subsequent three-year growth of real household consumption per person. This is a retrospective, descriptive country-year prediction, not a policy intervention or causal estimate.

## Strongest case

Inflation acceleration can erode real wages and purchasing power before nominal pay adjusts, and uncertainty can defer consumption. Real consumption per person is a household-facing flow, although it includes imputed items and does not measure the distribution of losses.

## Strongest alternative

Inflation may also accompany a demand recovery that raises real consumption, or a currency devaluation that raises export income. Simultaneous commodity, exchange-rate, and fiscal shocks can cause both. Country and year effects and lagged outcomes do not identify the inflation channel.
