# Private credit and manufacturing share, 2022–2024

## Strongest case for the claim

Credit depth can favour property, household consumption and existing assets
over new tradable production. A deep financial system may therefore be
associated with a falling manufacturing share even when aggregate output
grows. The old 1990–2023 panel reported a negative association, but it was a
same-commit legacy run and is known before this test.

## Strongest case against it

Credit can finance plants, inventory and export capacity. Manufacturing's GDP
share can also fall because service productivity, relative prices or income
change, without any loss of manufacturing output. Richer countries tend to
have both deeper credit and different sector mixes. A two-year change is noisy
and the WDI series can be revised. The fixed controls do not remove these
confounders or establish a policy effect.

## Fair adjudication

Use the 181 fixed countries and exact 2022/2024 WDI cells, with a lagged credit
exposure, baseline manufacturing share and baseline real income. The frozen
sample and variation floors must pass before reporting direction. A valid
estimate smaller than the declared magnitude or with a confidence interval
crossing zero is `PARTIAL`, not an excuse to change the country roster,
endpoint, or controls. Any measured result is descriptive calibration only.
