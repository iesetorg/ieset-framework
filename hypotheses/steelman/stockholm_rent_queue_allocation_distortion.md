# Steelman: stockholm_rent_queue_allocation_distortion

## Counter-argument

Queue length in Stockholm reflects revealed preference for a deeply
underpriced good in a capital-region labor market, not rationing
inefficiency: long queues are the predictable equilibrium of any
below-clearing price, and insiders' low mobility is a rational response to
losing a valuable contract, not a distortion per se. Comparison
municipalities differ in labor-market depth and amenity, so mobility gaps
may reflect opportunity structures rather than allocation rules.

## Strongest empirical objections

1. **Preference-driven queuing**: applicants queue years before needing a
   flat because the option is valuable; queue-time dispersion across
   districts may track neighborhood desirability, not allocation friction.

2. **Compositional mobility gaps**: Stockholm tenants are older, richer,
   and more established than the comparison-municipality average; without
   matching, the 15 percent mobility threshold may be a composition
   artifact.

3. **Completions are policy endogenous**: municipal housing companies
   build in response to political pressure from the queue, so completions
   failing to offset queue growth is partly a policy choice, not a market
   failure measure.

## What would change our mind

If cohort-district queue dispersion is flat after adjusting for
desirability fixed effects and matched-mobility gaps disappear, the
allocation-distortion layer reduces to "the queue prices the incumbent
transfer" without behavioral distortion — still informative for incidence,
but not for the allocation_distortion second-order layer.
