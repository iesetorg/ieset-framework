# Steelman: real_income_secondary_completion_holdout_v2

Among countries excluded from the old school-completion screen, higher real GDP per person at year t predicts higher attainment of at least upper-secondary education among adults age 25 and older at exact year t+1, with country and treatment-year effects.

Higher household and public resources can support longer schooling, while education itself can raise later income and the adult attainment stock changes slowly.

Attainment of at least upper-secondary education among adults age 25 and older is a slowly changing stock; income is not a market-policy intervention and schooling can also raise income. Country and treatment-year effects leave confounding and possible reverse causation. A result in either direction is a measured association, not a policy effect.

The old REFUTED result was already known; all 40 countries in that historical specification are excluded. The expected sign is fixed by the claim above. Support and refutation use the same two-sided 0.10/11 family threshold after a 400-pair/35-country complete-case gate and five pairs per country. Feasibility selection inspected null masks only, without outcome magnitudes or fits. No school claim receives score credit.
