# government_consumption_private_consumption_geographic_holdout_v2: steelman

## Strongest case

A larger government-consumption share might coincide with resource shifts or financing burdens that reduce later private consumption growth. The test compares measured macro series in countries absent from the old fit.

## Best contrary case

Government consumption is not a subsidy measure. Its share can rise in recessions when private GDP falls, and automatic responses can reverse the apparent direction. A negative coefficient would not establish crowding out or subsidy decay.

## Measurement boundary

WDI NE.CON.GOVT.ZS is government final consumption as a share of GDP, not subsidies. The outcome is measured private consumption growth. This association does not validate subsidy decay or causal crowding out.

## Frozen decision boundary

Every country in the historical 40-country old specification is excluded. The old PARTIAL result was known before this registration. The fixed 1996-2023 treatment window uses exact t and t+1 observations, finite complete cases, at least five pairs per country, and source files whose SHA-256 values match tracked fetch manifests. A five-test family requires at least 800 pairs and 50 countries, with two-sided p <= 0.02 for either predeclared or opposite significant direction. Other estimable results are PARTIAL; failed sample or estimator gates are INCONCLUSIVE. No result is available at registration. Any eventual result is an associational calibration with zero school or causal credit.
