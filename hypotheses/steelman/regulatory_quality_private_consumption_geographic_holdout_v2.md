# regulatory_quality_private_consumption_geographic_holdout_v2: steelman

## Strongest case

Clearer and more predictable regulation may ease private production and spending. The new test asks whether the observed regulatory-quality estimate predicts subsequent measured household consumption growth in countries absent from the old fit.

## Best contrary case

Regulatory-quality perceptions can respond to income and expectations. Household consumption reflects saving, credit, external shocks, and measurement choices. A positive coefficient would remain an association and could reflect omitted trends.

## Measurement boundary

WGI regulatory quality is a perceived-governance estimate, not observed market freedom; household final consumption per person is the measured endpoint. This is a measured association and does not validate the old market-freedom or causal label.

## Frozen decision boundary

Every country in the historical 40-country old specification is excluded. The old SUPPORTED result was known before this registration. The fixed 1996-2023 treatment window uses exact t and t+1 observations, finite complete cases, at least five pairs per country, and source files whose SHA-256 values match tracked fetch manifests. A five-test family requires at least 800 pairs and 50 countries, with two-sided p <= 0.02 for either predeclared or opposite significant direction. Other estimable results are PARTIAL; failed sample or estimator gates are INCONCLUSIVE. No result is available at registration. Any eventual result is an associational calibration with zero school or causal credit.
