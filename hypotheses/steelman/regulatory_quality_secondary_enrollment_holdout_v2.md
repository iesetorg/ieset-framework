# Steelman: regulatory_quality_secondary_enrollment_holdout_v2

Among countries excluded from the old private-school-entry screen, a higher World Governance Indicators regulatory-quality estimate at year t predicts a higher secondary-school gross enrollment ratio at exact year t+1, conditional on real income per person and country and treatment-year effects.

A more predictable institutional environment might support education provision and household income, while education policy and development can cause both the score and enrollment to rise.

Regulatory quality does not measure private-school entry; gross enrollment can exceed 100 percent and does not measure teaching quality. Country and treatment-year effects plus baseline income leave confounding and possible reverse causation. A result in either direction is a measured association, not a policy effect.

The old PARTIAL result was already known; all 40 countries in that historical specification are excluded. The expected sign is fixed by the claim above. Support and refutation use the same two-sided 0.10/11 family threshold after a 400-pair/35-country complete-case gate and five pairs per country. Feasibility selection inspected null masks only, without outcome magnitudes or fits. No school claim receives score credit.
