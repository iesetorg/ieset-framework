# Steelman: statutory corporate tax changes and net FDI inflows

A lower combined statutory corporate income tax rate can increase the expected after-tax return on mobile international capital. The frozen question is whether an annual rate reduction predicts a higher **two-year forward change** in WDI foreign direct investment net inflows as a share of GDP. The claim is an association, not a policy effect.

The OECD series is a combined statutory headline rate, not the effective tax burden faced by a particular investment. WDI FDI is a balance-of-payments **net inflow** ratio and can be negative or volatile; it does not isolate greenfield projects, local jobs, or productive capital. Source definitions: [OECD corporate income tax rates](https://www.oecd.org/en/data/datasets/corporate-income-tax-rates-database.html), [World Bank FDI net inflows indicator](https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/BX.KLT.DINV.WD.GD.ZS).

The strongest alternative is that financial-centre routing, mergers, global rates, country shocks, GDP changes, and simultaneous reform packages move both variables. Country and year effects do not remove those mechanisms. The time ordering is descriptive and does not identify a tax reform effect.

The exact sample, 0.2 GDP-share percentage-point per CIT percentage-point meaningful effect, country/year FE, minimum sample and material-rate-change floors, two-sided p <= 0.005, and three-way verdict rule are fixed in the linked specification and family registration before outcome access. Negative, null, positive, missing, and inconclusive results must be retained. There is no school, policy, causal, or featured credit.
