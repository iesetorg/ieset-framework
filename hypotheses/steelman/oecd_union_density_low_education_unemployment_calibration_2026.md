# Union density and low-education unemployment: steelman

Collective bargaining can raise wages and improve working conditions for covered workers. If it raises hiring costs for outsiders, economies with higher union density could also see slower employment recovery among adults with low educational attainment. The registered test asks whether ten additional percentage points of union density at an anchor year predict a larger change in low-education unemployment over the following two years, conditional on the starting unemployment rate, real GDP per capita, country effects, and common anchor-year shocks.

The strongest competing explanation is that weak labour demand can raise unemployment and change union membership at the same time. Union density also differs from coverage: extension rules can cover nonmembers, and density can rise when nonunion jobs disappear. The six two-year windows are separated by one year within each country so outcome periods do not overlap. Country and anchor-year effects remove stable differences and common shocks, but cannot remove all time-varying confounding or isolate a policy effect.

The result can only calibrate the **low-education adult unemployment** component. It does not test youth unemployment or wage compression. The archived `union_density_wage_compression_employment_tradeoff` Fraser-EFW/GDP-per-capita placeholder stays archived, with no score or verdict transfer. A null or contrary estimate must be retained. The single registered two-sided p threshold is 0.01, with a practical magnitude of 0.25 unemployment percentage point per ten points of union density, subject to all sample and influence floors. No causal, school, policy, or featured credit is assigned.
