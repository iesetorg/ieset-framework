# Internet uptake and later resident patent filings: steelman

A wider online network may reduce search, collaboration, and technical-document access costs for local inventors. If that mechanism is large enough to show in national filing counts, a two-year increase in the share of people using the Internet at year `t` should be followed by higher resident patent applications per million from `t` to `t+2`. The primary estimand is a descriptive within-country association after country and treatment-year effects, baseline patent intensity, and baseline real income.

The rival account is strong: Internet-use estimates are not broadband quality or productive use; much adoption is for consumption. Rising patents can precede wider digital uptake, and income, industrial composition, legal reforms, filing practices, and common technology shocks can move both. A negative association could arise if innovation shifts toward software or trade secrets that are less likely to be filed domestically. The exact WIPO-origin patent count is a WDI API mirror, not an independently measured outcome.

The source and key roster, country/year window, two-year ordering, complete-case and exposure-concentration floors, practical magnitude, and local two-test p≤0.005 cutoff must be committed before outcome values or null masks are read. A positive beta below the significance or practical gate is PARTIAL, not support. A negative beta beyond both gates refutes only the directional association. This test cannot establish an internet or spectrum policy effect, improve school scores, or be featured as a causal finding.
