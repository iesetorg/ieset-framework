# Steelman — export-market concentration and later export-share volatility

A country that sells merchandise mainly into a few destination markets is
more exposed to partner-specific recessions, sanctions, tariffs, or demand
changes. An increase in the WITS market Herfindahl-Hirschman index from 2000
to 2010 might therefore precede larger swings in exports as a share of GDP
during 2011–2020. The prior export-share volatility and 2010 export/GDP
level controls temper simple persistence and scale explanations.

The strongest objection is that this outcome is not a direct test of partner
concentration. WDI exports include services whereas the WITS index measures
merchandise partner concentration. The WDI denominator is GDP; a domestic
recession can lift the export ratio even with flat export value. Product
specialisation, commodity cycles, exchange rates, country size, income,
and 2020 pandemic shocks can affect both series. The prior controls cannot
remove those channels, and market concentration can itself respond to
expected volatility.

The honest result is a descriptive forward calibration. A positive
association would be compatible with the destination-risk mechanism but
would not establish it. A null or negative association should be reported
under the same fixed thresholds. Neither result should be used as policy,
causal, school, position, or featured evidence.
