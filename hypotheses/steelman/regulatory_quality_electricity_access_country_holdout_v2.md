# Regulatory quality and next-year electricity access

## Why a positive association is plausible

Regulatory quality may reflect capable public administration and predictable rules that help electricity providers connect households. A one-year lead in the access measure gives a narrow descriptive ordering, and the fixed country and year effects plus starting income and rule of law reduce some obvious cross-country differences.

## Why the result could fail or mislead

Electricity access is a percentage with a hard 100% ceiling. Countries already near universal access may provide little usable within-country variation. Governance estimates are broad perceptions, and electrification can improve for reasons they do not measure, including public investment, donor finance, technology, and geography. Income and rule-of-law controls cannot remove time-varying confounding or reverse causation. The old candidate mislabeled regulatory quality as user pricing and access as maintenance quality; its same-commit PARTIAL result is not confirmation. An adverse or statistically unresolved coefficient is a meaningful outcome.

## Frozen test and decision

Use the four pinned publisher snapshots in the specification, a fixed 167-code roster entirely outside the old 40-country fit, exact t to t+1 joins, a five-year rule, a complete-case floor of 800 pairs and 50 codes, and country/year fixed effects with country-clustered inference. The 84 screened source-pair opportunities define a two-sided Bonferroni threshold of 0.1/84. Preserve INCONCLUSIVE when the sample or estimator fails; preserve PARTIAL when the threshold is missed. This is descriptive calibration only, with no policy, causal, user-pricing, maintenance, or school-score credit.
