# Nordic cash redistribution and the Gini gap, 2027–2029

## Strongest case

The social-democratic model combines progressive direct taxes and cash benefits with broad labor-market participation. In the Nordic economies, that architecture should continue to produce a substantial gap between the dispersion of market income and disposable household income. The forecast fixes all five Nordic countries, all three future income years, one OECD IDD definition, and a 0.10-Gini support threshold before the selected data exist.

## Strongest alternative

The difference between the two Ginis is an accounting comparison, not the causal effect of taxes and transfers. Household labor supply, pension classification, changing age composition, market-income dispersion, survey revisions, and the exclusion of in-kind services and indirect taxes all affect interpretation. A narrower gap could reflect better market incomes rather than weaker redistribution. The measured gap alone cannot establish that the Nordic system improves growth, welfare, or mobility.

## Evidence boundary

The registered result is descriptive and depends on one future OECD IDD release with all 15 exact country-year pairs. Missing observations, method breaks, or source-custody failures are inconclusive. No run or school score exists at registration.
