# wits_export_concentration_hightech_growth_geographic_holdout_v2: steelman

## Strongest case

A narrow export basket can signal capabilities concentrated outside high-technology goods. The following year's growth in high-technology export value offers a narrower, country-disjoint check of whether the measured relationship travels.

## Best contrary case

Successful specialization can raise concentration, while high-technology exports can be concentrated in a few products. Both indicators use trade records, so some underlying data are shared. Current-dollar growth also reflects prices and exchange rates. This design cannot establish innovation, industrial upgrading, or a policy effect.

## Frozen decision boundary

The old SUPPORTED result was known before registration. All 39 countries in the old specification are excluded. Only exact t and t+1 observations from source-pinned vintages count. At least five years per country, 500 complete pairs, and 40 countries are required. This two-test family uses a two-sided p<=0.05 threshold for both negative support and positive refutation; otherwise an estimable result is PARTIAL, and a failed gate is INCONCLUSIVE. No result exists at registration. Any result is descriptive calibration with zero school credit.
