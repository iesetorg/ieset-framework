# Steelman — US state minimum-wage bite and teen employment

A wage floor can affect the hiring margin for inexperienced workers more than
for the full workforce. A state bite ratio uses a local median wage denominator
and therefore speaks more directly to labour-market exposure than the nominal
statutory floor. An age-16–19 employment-to-population outcome is a closer
descriptive match to the archived teen-employment questions than an all-age
ratio or youth unemployment rate. State and year fixed effects remove stable
state differences and common national shocks.

The opposite account is substantial: higher state wage floors may follow
stronger local labour markets; the median wage denominator moves with local
conditions; teen employment also changes with school attendance, migration,
seasonal work and pandemic recovery. ACS one-year state ratios have sampling
error and cannot be interpreted as the employment response of a treated group.
The 2014–2018 and 2021/2023–2024 panels omit years because the pinned wage
source lacks 2019 and 2022 and a standard ACS one-year table is absent in
2020. A sign change when refitting 2014–2018 prevents a directional verdict.
Residualized treatment cannot be driven by one state.

The study is a single, selected, correlated descriptive calibration. It does
not satisfy the archived five-case DiD design, estimate a policy effect,
award school points, or turn the age-specific result into an independent
confirmation of the existing all-age and international youth results.
