# Resident patents and later manufacturing hourly productivity

Patent applications can proxy an inventive pipeline whose practical adoption
takes time. A three-year forward mean of manufacturing real GVA per hour growth
is a direct, narrower test of that productivity channel than the earlier
patents-to-current-dollar-high-tech-export-value study. The WIPO resident
series is divided by contemporaneous population to avoid treating country
size as invention intensity.

The strongest objection is selection and timing: productive firms can file
more patents before a growth phase, patent propensity varies by law and sector,
and many filings never become productive innovations. The OECD PDB outcome can
also change through hours and national-account revisions. Country and year
effects, current manufacturing productivity growth, and forward ordering do
not remove these causes. A positive association is calibration only, not proof
that patent policy raises manufacturing productivity.

The old `wipo_patent_applications_hightech_export_followthrough_panel` result
and its strict high-tech export-value follow-up were visible before this
registration. The new primary outcome is manufacturing GVA per hour growth;
no verdict or school link carries over. A significant negative coefficient
under the frozen two-test family rule refutes this measured positive claim.
