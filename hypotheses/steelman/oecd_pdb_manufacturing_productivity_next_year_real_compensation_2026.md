# Manufacturing productivity and next-year real compensation — steelman

The positive claim is that higher real value-added-per-hour growth in manufacturing is followed by higher sector labour compensation per employee after national CPI inflation. A demand expansion or wage bargain could share productivity gains with workers. Capital gains, weaker bargaining, changes in hours, worker selection or lagged demand can break the link or reverse it. A qualified negative estimate refutes this directional claim.

The test uses the frozen OECD PDB GVAHRS GY series at t, PDB LCEMP nominal GY and WDI CPI at t and t+1. It controls prior real pay growth and country/year effects. CPI is a national consumer basket, not a sector deflator; compensation per employee is not the median worker's wage. All three sectors share sources, years and the model, so they form one correlated family.

Visibility requires committed preregistration, three source locks, measured complete-case coverage, residual variation and concentration gates, country-cluster inference, three-test Holm correction, and sign stability under hash-fold omissions. This descriptive design cannot score a school, position, policy, causal claim or featured result.
