# Steelman: welfare_anglo_transfer_ratchet_private_stagnation_2019_2024

## Counter-argument

Public-spending shares that fail to fall back reflect slow recoveries and
publication lags, not political irreversibility: 2024 WDI values are
missing for several units, the "peak" is pandemic-era emergency spending
that unwinds with multi-year program horizons, and the ratchet test on a
share with a depressed denominator mechanically understates give-backs.
The voter-lockin mechanism is untested narrative — transfer systems have
been retrenched under stress before (Canada 1995, UK 2010-2015 austerity),
just not within this six-year window.

## Strongest empirical objections

1. **Precedent of retrenchment**: both Canada (1990s program review) and
   the UK (post-2010 consolidation) cut shares by multiple points under
   fiscal stress; the claim that ratchets never release is window-dependent.

2. **USA contrast overread**: US public-consumption give-back reflects its
   different cyclical timing (earlier reopening), not a deeper political
   economy difference.

3. **Expense-share data quality**: GC.XPN.TOTL.GD.ZS has gaps and
   definitional breaks across the five countries; the ratchet measure is
   sensitive to which of the two share series carries a country.

## What would change our mind

Canada or Australia returning their public-consumption share to within
one point of 2019 levels by 2025-2026, or the UK's 2024-2025 outturns
showing a two-point consolidation without a bond event, would move this
family toward PARTIAL and falsify the strong version of the paralysis
reading.
