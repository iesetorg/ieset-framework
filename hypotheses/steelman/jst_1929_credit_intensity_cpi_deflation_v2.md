# Steelman — 1929 credit intensity and price-level loss

When private credit contracts, spending and money creation may weaken, making
price deflation more severe where 1929 loans were large relative to output.
The strongest empirical version predicts a larger 1929–1933 CPI index loss in
the high-credit countries of the fixed historical panel. A strong alternative
is that gold-standard policy, exchange-rate adjustment, fiscal stance, and
international demand determined deflation; credit intensity could simply mark
financial development. A rank correlation cannot establish the direction of
causation or a monetary-policy effect.

The real-output test is a second, distinct measurement of the same
interwar-vulnerability question. The two tests share one Bonferroni denominator
and do not count as independent replication. No policy or school score follows.
