# Pre-1914 broad money and inflation in JST R6

The archived `friedman_inflation_always_monetary_long_run_panel` asks whether
long-run excess broad-money growth moves with inflation in 1960–2023. Its
same-commit result cannot count as a prospective result. The 1870–1910 JST
decades provide a separate historical first-look sample and a direct broad
money, real output, and consumer-price construct from one source.

A positive slope near one would be consistent with a long-run monetary
regularity, but it would not identify the direction of causation. Nominal
money responds to prices, output, and banking conditions; money velocity can
change. The four prewar decades also represent advanced economies under
distinct national arrangements, yet mostly one broad era. A weak or negative
within-country association would limit the archived claim's historical reach.

The frozen registration permits exactly one fixed-effects slope, country
cluster uncertainty, and country-deletion sign diagnostics. The support range,
sample and concentration floors were set before money, CPI, output, or
population values or null masks were opened. Inadequate measured coverage or
variation is INCONCLUSIVE. No result earns causal, policy, school, position,
or featured credit. The JST data are licensed CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 and are not
redistributed in this repository.
