# Steelman: prior destination growth and RO-citizenship immigration

Rising real income per person may signal better destination opportunity, and established migrant networks may transmit that signal before subsequent moves. The fixed prior growth and later count-change windows make the descriptive prediction testable.

The competing explanation is destination policy, registration practice, family reunification, origin-specific events, migration networks and changing legal status. People with a given citizenship can move from any prior residence; citizenship is not departure country. Small and zero flows can yield unstable percentage changes, so the frozen material-flow gate and diagnostics must be enforced. The 30 streams reuse destination growth and are correlated. No result identifies a GDP-growth or migration-policy effect.
