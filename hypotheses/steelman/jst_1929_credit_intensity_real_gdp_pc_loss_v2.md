# Steelman — 1929 credit intensity and real output loss

A highly leveraged banking system may have less room to absorb falling asset
values and loan defaults, so economies with more private credit relative to
output in 1929 could experience sharper real per-person output losses through
1933. The strongest empirical version requires this prediction to hold across
the fixed advanced-economy roster, not just in the United States. A strong
counterargument is that precrisis credit intensity also measures financial
depth, while exchange-rate regime, trade exposure, monetary and fiscal
responses, and initial industrial structure govern Depression losses. A
country-level rank correlation cannot distinguish these mechanisms.

The price-level follow-up is a second, distinct measurement of the same
interwar-vulnerability question. The two tests share one Bonferroni denominator
and do not count as independent replication. No policy or school score follows.
