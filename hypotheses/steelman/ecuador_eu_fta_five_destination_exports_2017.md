# Steelman — Ecuador–EU agreement, five-destination exports, 2017

The [Official Journal notice](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:22016X1229(02)) says that Ecuador's accession protocol, signed 11 November 2016, was provisionally applied from 1 January 2017. The [European Commission's Andean trade page](https://policy.trade.ec.europa.eu/eu-trade-relationships-country-and-region/countries-and-regions/andean-community_en) confirms that timing. Preferential access and more predictable rules could increase Ecuador-origin goods exports to EU members, even if firms had anticipated the agreement.

The primary contrast treats DEU, ESP, FRA, ITA, and NLD as one fixed EU-destination portfolio. ARG, BOL, BRA, PRY, and URY are fixed South American comparator origins to each destination. CEPII records no EU agreement for those donor dyads in 2012–2019, while Ecuador's status switches from 0 to 1 in 2017. CHL, COL, and PER are excluded because their EU agreement status is already 1 in the comparison period. VEN is excluded from this comparator portfolio because its contemporaneous macroeconomic collapse makes it a poor donor. This is a policy-relevant geographic comparison, not five destination-level hypotheses.

Earlier IESET treaty tests used this CEPII publisher and BACI source family; source-wide blindness is not claimed. No Ecuador–EU five-destination tradeflow value was inspected for this registration.

The strongest contrary interpretation is that the agreement's marginal tariff change was small: the EU maintained a temporary preference bridge for Ecuador in 2015–2016, as documented in the [European Commission's 2014 proposal](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:52014PC0585). Earthquake recovery, commodity prices, euro and dollar conditions, and destination-specific product mix could produce the same contrast. CEPII's BACI measure is nominal value, not physical export volume or welfare. A passing result is descriptive calibration only.
