# Steelman: top statutory personal income tax and disposable-income Gini

The strongest case for the claim is that a higher top statutory rate can reduce high-end after-tax wage income and, if enforced without offsetting changes, lower measured dispersion of disposable household income. The fixed test asks only whether a rate hike of at least one percentage point in year t is associated with a lower change in the OECD all-age disposable-income Gini from t to t+1 than a stable-rate year. Tax cuts are a separate nuisance category.

The strongest counter is measurement and policy mismatch. This OECD rate concerns the top bracket of gross wage earnings, not the effective tax paid by high-income households; thresholds, deductions, capital-income treatment, social contributions, enforcement, and other taxes and transfers can change simultaneously. High earners may shift the timing or form of reported income. A broad Gini moves with the whole distribution and may barely respond to a top-bracket change. National surveys and OECD harmonisation can change the measured Gini. Reforms may be responses to inequality or recessions; country and year effects and forward ordering do not identify a tax effect. The model omits a separate baseline-Gini covariate because Gini at t is already the subtraction endpoint and may follow the tax change at t. This does not eliminate regression to the mean or confounding by starting inequality.

The test uses exact pinned OECD vintages, a committed country/year roster built from tax values and Gini keys alone, a separate committed source lock, and one fixed-effects regression. Finite Gini endpoints in [0,1] with accepted status A are required after lock. Failed source, sample, event, concentration, rank, or inference gates are inconclusive. Meaningful support or refutation requires a 0.005 Gini-unit coefficient magnitude and two-sided p at most 0.005; otherwise the result is partial.

Related earlier top-PIT work on WID pretax top-income shares and WDI business registrations, and an earlier union-density/IDD-Gini run, were visible before registration. The current comparison is a new exact source/measure pairing but not an independent archive-wide holdout. Even a supported association earns no causal, policy, school, position, or featured credit.
