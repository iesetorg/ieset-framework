# Electric-power use and forward power/heat CO2: strongest case and limits

Higher electricity consumption can require more generation and therefore more fuel combustion when the marginal supply is fossil. WDI electric-power consumption per capita measures net power use in kWh per person, while Eurostat's CRF1A1A inventory records territorial public electricity and heat-production CO2. A positive forward association is plausible but far from mechanical because net consumption and domestic public generation are different quantities.

Electricity can be imported, exported, generated by nuclear or renewable sources, or supplied by private/autoproducer facilities outside the chosen inventory category. Per-capita use can change because population changes. Heat production and fuel mix can dominate the inventory outcome. Economic activity, weather, hydro output, fuel prices, and ETS rules can move both series. The 2020 pandemic and 2022 energy crisis make full-period results particularly vulnerable. IEA-origin WDI values and EEA inventory estimates have distinct revision and reporting processes; the pinned files do not provide observation flags sufficient to identify individual breaks.

The registered sample uses exact annual matches, caps unusually large log changes, requires broad material exposure, uses country and treatment-year fixed effects with country-cluster uncertainty, and repeats the fit on pre-2019 treatment years. This remains a measured forward association with zero policy, causal, school-score, and featured credit. It is not a test of an electricity-pricing or generation policy.
