# Steelman: Spain's 2024–25 macro trajectory against fixed euro-area peers

The strongest narrow case is that Spain's 2024-to-2025 growth in real output per resident and its change in the employment rate both compare at least as favorably with the median of seven prespecified euro-area peers. Direct Eurostat national accounts, population, and labour-force series allow the same definitions across the eight countries. The two held-out years are outside the old Spain study's 2005–2023 outcome sample; they are not claimed to be globally unseen by IESET. A prior Poland run used the same Eurostat population vintage for its older sample. The old Spain result was **PARTIAL**, and its housing and real-wage legs were missing; neither fact can be revised by this follow-up.

The strongest rival account is that a favorable Spanish gap could come from tourism, migration, labour composition, recovery timing, or country-specific shocks. The peer median removes neither those factors nor policy differences among peers. A one-year change gives no reliable estimate of the 2018 government's effect. A weak or adverse GDP or employment leg must be reported under the joint rule, including a mixed result when the two disagree.

The frozen test uses only the 2024 and 2025 values in three source-pinned Eurostat series. It computes Spain's log real-GDP-per-resident change and employment-rate percentage-point change, then subtracts the median corresponding changes of FRA, ITA, PRT, GRC, DEU, NLD, and BEL. Both gaps at or above zero support the descriptive claim; both below zero refute it; split signs are partial. This test gives no causal credit to any government and does not settle the older housing or real-wage claim.
