# Steelman: OECD employment protection and later unemployment duration

Stricter legal protection can lengthen unemployed workers' spells if firms become slower to hire into jobs that are costly to end. The 2023 OECD regular-worker EPR index is a direct measure of this contract margin. Eurostat's `une_ltu_a` counts spells of at least 12 months; dividing its rate by the same-age total unemployment rate produces the fraction of unemployed people with long spells. A 2025 endpoint is later than the old run's 2023 limit and is not derived from the old mislabelled education-by-region rate.

The counterargument is substantial. Dismissal protection can reduce separations as well as hiring, and differences in benefit duration, activation policy, skill composition, recession timing, and survey rules affect the long-term fraction. Country institutional bundles can drive both EPL and duration. Baseline adjustment cannot remove these confounders; 2025 may be revised. A small cross-country association is therefore calibration only. Null or opposite findings must be reported with the same prominence as a positive one. The old labour result cannot be treated as prior verification because its named duration outcome was actually general unemployment.
