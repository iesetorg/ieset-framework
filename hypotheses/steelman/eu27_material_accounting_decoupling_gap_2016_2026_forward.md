# EU-27 material-accounting decoupling gap, 2016–2026

## Strongest case for the forecast

Domestic material consumption (DMC) records material directly extracted and traded by an economy. Raw material consumption (RMC) estimates the extraction embodied in the goods and services its residents consume, including material used abroad. If growing EU economies relocate material-intensive stages of production or increase imports of processed goods, DMC can fall while RMC does not. A country can therefore appear to decouple output from materials under DMC accounting without achieving consumption-based decoupling.

## Strongest alternative

Efficiency gains, reuse, product substitution, and lower-material consumption can reduce *both* DMC and RMC while real income rises. Conversely, differences between the measures can reflect construction cycles, fuel imports, trade composition, population changes, or modelling choices rather than offshored production. The predicted ten-point country-share gap may be absent in 2026. A small or opposite gap refutes this narrow forecast; it does not prove that GDP growth is ecologically sustainable or that degrowth policy is harmful.

## Evidence boundary

This is one descriptive comparison of fixed 2016 and 2026 endpoints for the same 27 EU countries, conditional on real GDP per person increasing. It will use Eurostat [RMC material footprints](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/databrowser/product/view/env_ac_rme), [DMC material flow accounts](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/databrowser/product/view/env_ac_mfa), and [real GDP per capita](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/databrowser/product/view/sdg_08_10), with the first complete future source vintage retained. Eurostat explains that country RMC figures may be estimated and are not additive to the EU aggregate in its [RMC metadata](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/cache/metadata/EN/env_ac_rme_esms.htm). The 2026 RMC endpoint is not yet available at registration. No study run or school result exists.
