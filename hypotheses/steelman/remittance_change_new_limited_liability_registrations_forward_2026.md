# Remittance change and later limited-liability registrations: rival explanations

Some recipient households may use transfers to finance deposits, equipment,
working capital, or incorporation fees. If that formal-entry channel is
material, a larger remittance-share increase could precede higher growth
in new limited-liability registrations the following calendar year.

An alternative is that remittances mainly smooth consumption, finance
housing, or support informal microbusinesses; none requires limited-liability
registration. International migration can also remove potential founders
from the origin economy. A recession can raise the remittance-to-GDP ratio
through its denominator while lowering registrations, creating an adverse
association without implying transfers deter enterprise.

The registration source counts legal entries, not operating firms. Registry
digitization, a mandatory incorporation rule, legal-form substitution,
administrative backlogs, and changes in reporting coverage can shift counts
without an economic entry response. Country and year effects plus GDP
controls cannot establish a remittance-induced business effect. The
registration endpoint is reused by an IESET tax family, so this result is
not an independent replication of that study.

The fixed design retains zero and large valid counts through log1p, reports
missing or flagged endpoint years, and keeps contrary results. A null test
does not exclude informal entrepreneurship. No causal, policy, school, or
featured credit is available.
