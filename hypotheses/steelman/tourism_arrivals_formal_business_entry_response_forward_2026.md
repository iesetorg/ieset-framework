# Steelman: tourism arrivals and new formal limited-liability registrations per million residents

A visitor-demand increase can transmit through spending at hotels, transport, food services, construction, suppliers, and household income. If the channel is strong, stronger arrivals growth in year t should precede a stronger next-year increase in new formal limited-liability registrations per million residents. The frozen model compares countries within themselves while accounting for common calendar-year shifts, baseline outcome, and income level.

The strongest alternative is reverse timing or a shared shock. Growth, currency depreciation, airline routes, border rules, global demand, security, and statistical revisions can jointly move arrivals and the response. A measured null or opposite association would weaken this directed prediction but would not establish a causal harm from tourism. Registration is a formal legal event, not an operating firm, local tourism business, or job.

This is one of six preregistered response margins. The endpoints are economically distinct but use overlapping WDI years and the same exposure; the family-wide Bonferroni cutoff is 0.01/6. A failed sample or estimator gate is INCONCLUSIVE; an estimable weak result is PARTIAL. No causal, policy, school, position, or featured credit.
