# Steelman against a Korea–EU5 export contrast

The strongest rival explanation is Korean export competitiveness rather than
the 2011 treaty. Automobile and electronics product cycles, exchange rates,
and the global recovery after 2008 could move Korea's nominal exports to
Europe relative to a mixed Asian donor set. The 2012 KOR–USA agreement also
overlaps the post window and could reallocate production or trade routes.

The five destinations are fixed before reading their BACI values, but they do
not cover every EU member and Germany may dominate the sum. Median donor
changes absorb some destination-wide shocks; they cannot make source countries
exchangeable. The 2007–2010 pretrend screen may fail because the crisis hit
countries and sectors differently. Failure must remain INCONCLUSIVE, without
choosing a different period or donor list. Even a passing contrast describes
nominal goods-flow divergence only, with no tariff intensity, real volume,
employment, growth, welfare, or causal interpretation. Existing FTA screens
make this a selected exploratory case rather than a fresh independent
replication.
