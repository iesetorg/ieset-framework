# Steelman: tax_revenue_corruption_control_holdout_v2

Among countries excluded from the old tax-complexity screen, higher total tax revenue as a share of GDP at year t predicts a lower World Governance Indicators control-of-corruption estimate at exact year t+1, conditional on real income per person and country and treatment-year effects.

A larger revenue take can expand discretionary fiscal rents, while capable states can instead collect more tax and police corruption more effectively. The negative sign tests only the first possibility.

Total tax receipts do not measure tax-code complexity; the WGI estimate is a perception composite, not direct public trust. Country and treatment-year effects plus baseline income leave confounding and possible reverse causation. A result in either direction is a measured association, not a policy effect.

The old PARTIAL result was already known; all 40 countries in that historical specification are excluded. The expected sign is fixed by the claim above. Support and refutation use the same two-sided 0.10/11 family threshold after a 400-pair/35-country complete-case gate and five pairs per country. Feasibility selection inspected null masks only, without outcome magnitudes or fits. No school claim receives score credit.
