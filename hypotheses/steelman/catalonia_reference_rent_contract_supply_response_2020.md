# Steelman: catalonia_reference_rent_contract_supply_response_2020

## Counter-argument

Catalonia's reference-price system had documented enforcement gaps and
legal challenges; designation may correlate with pre-existing rental-market
tightness that was already reversing by 2021 (post-pandemic urban rent
correction), so contract-volume declines in designated municipalities may
reflect the 2020-2021 demand shock and the later tourism-recovery
reaspiration of stock into seasonal use, not the cap. Landlord exit may be
a story about regional fiscal and legal uncertainty (the 2019-2022
constitutional disputes over Catalan housing competence) rather than rent
reference levels.

## Strongest empirical objections

1. **Designation endogeneity**: stressed-area designation is a function of
   prior rent growth and supply pressure — exactly the variables that
   predict subsequent mean reversion in contract volumes.

2. **Informal substitution runs both ways**: a measured formal-contract
   decline could partly be reclassification (tourist and seasonal use)
   that would have happened anyway post-pandemic.

3. **Second wave contamination**: Spain Law 12/2023 reaches nominally
   untreated comparison municipalities by 2023, eroding the control group
   in the final two sample years.

## What would change our mind

If non-designated border municipalities show the same contract-volume
trajectory (common regional shock), or if the decline is concentrated in
categories the cap never bound, the supply-response leg fails and the
European rent-control evidence reduces to the incidence-only result.
