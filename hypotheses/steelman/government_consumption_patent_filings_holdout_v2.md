# Steelman: government consumption patent filings holdout v2

Among countries excluded from the old state-allocation study, a higher general-government final-consumption share of GDP at year t predicts fewer resident patent applications per million people at exact year t+1, conditional on baseline real income per person and country and treatment-year effects.

Government final consumption is not a direct measure of subsidy allocation or public R&D; resident patent filings do not measure invention quality or welfare. Country and year effects plus baseline income leave omitted causes and reverse causation. A result in either direction is only a measured association.

The old REFUTED result was known, and all 41 countries in that historical specification are excluded. The new direction is fixed by the claim above. Expected and adverse coefficients face the same five-test two-sided threshold of p<=0.02 after a 400-pair/35-country complete-case gate. No school claim receives score credit.
