# Steelman: total_tax_revenue_labor_participation_holdout_v2

Among countries excluded from the old payroll-tax screen, higher total tax revenue as a share of GDP at year t predicts lower labour-force participation at exact year t+1, conditional on real income per person and country and treatment-year effects.

A larger tax take could correspond to labour wedges that discourage participation; it could also fund childcare, health care, or income support that raises participation.

Tax revenue as a share of GDP is not a payroll-tax rate; participation is not hours worked or employment. Country and treatment-year effects plus baseline income leave confounding and possible reverse causation. A result in either direction is a measured association, not a policy effect.

The old PARTIAL result was already known; all 40 countries in that historical specification are excluded. The expected sign is fixed by the claim above. Support and refutation use the same two-sided 0.10/11 family threshold after a 400-pair/35-country complete-case gate and five pairs per country. Feasibility selection inspected null masks only, without outcome magnitudes or fits. No school claim receives score credit.
