# Steelman: el_salvador_bukele_security_growth_equilibrium_2019_2026

## Counter-argument

El Salvador's growth record 2019-2024 is dominated by the 2021 pandemic
rebound (+11.9 percent); era means that include 2020 symmetrically flatter
both sides, and excluding the rebound years the growth premium over peers
is thin. The homicide collapse was achieved by negotiated gang truces and
mass detention (roughly two percent of the adult population incarcerated),
either of which can unwind; FDI recovered in percent-of-GDP terms but
remains below the regional frontier, and credit depth held rather than
deepened — consistent with security rents accruing to incumbents, not new
entry. Remittance stability may reflect deportation-cycle demographics
rather than a healthy equilibrium.

## Strongest empirical objections

1. **Rebound arithmetic**: 2019-2024 means lean on 2021; a 2022-2024
   window shows growth converging to, not beating, peers.

2. **Sustainability of enforcement**: the equilibrium's fiscal leg
   (deficit, IMF program, bitcoin-law contingent liabilities) is excluded
   here but is the documented fragility; a bond-stress event would falsify
   durability from outside this metric set.

3. **Measurement environment**: WDI homicide series for SLV ends 2022 in
   the current vintages; later national figures (single-digit rates) are
   government-published with limited independent verification.

## What would change our mind

Homicide rates reverting above 15 per 100k, FDI falling back below the
2010s mean for two consecutive years, or credit depth breaking below the
minus-five-point band would each break the equilibrium claim even if the
headline growth premium survives.
