# Steelman: weighted tariff and real consumption growth

Lower tariff rates can accompany cheaper imports and stronger household purchasing power. A country-disjoint panel tests whether the measured tariff rate at t has the expected negative relation to next-year real private-consumption-per-person growth.

Trade policy is endogenous to shocks and can coincide with fiscal, exchange-rate, and industrial changes. WITS here mirrors a World Bank tariff indicator, while real consumption uses a WDI constant-currency series. A significant association cannot identify tariff liberalisation or welfare causation.

The old 40-country REFUTED result is disclosed, and every country in its specification is excluded. The new test uses a fixed four-test two-sided Bonferroni threshold, five treatment years per country, and symmetric adverse-direction refutation. If complete cases or the estimator fail, it is INCONCLUSIVE. No school claim receives credit.
