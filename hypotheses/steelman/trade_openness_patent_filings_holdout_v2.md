# Steelman: trade openness patent filings holdout v2

Among countries excluded from the old innovation-diffusion study, a higher goods-and-services trade share of GDP at year t predicts more resident patent applications per million people at exact year t+1, conditional on baseline real income per person and country and treatment-year effects.

Trade as a share of GDP is not a tariff or import-knowledge measure; patent filings do not measure patent quality and may reflect reporting institutions. Country and year effects plus baseline income leave omitted causes and reverse causation. A result in either direction is only a measured association.

The old PARTIAL result was known, and all 41 countries in that historical specification are excluded. The new direction is fixed by the claim above. Expected and adverse coefficients face the same five-test two-sided threshold of p<=0.02 after a 400-pair/35-country complete-case gate. No school claim receives score credit.
