# Legal-system improvement and later resident patent filings

Clearer property rights, more reliable courts and stronger legal protection can make investment in formal inventions and patent filing more worthwhile. If broad legal institutions improve, domestic residents may file more applications per person during the next three years. This is a plausible mechanism, not a claim that the Fraser index measures patent law itself.

The strongest rival account is joint modernization: higher income, industrial change, research spending and administrative capacity can improve measured institutions and filings together. Changes in patent-office practice, international filing incentives and country coverage can change application counts without a change in inventive output. Even a positive result would identify a descriptive association only. Baseline filing intensity, baseline real income and country/year effects address some persistent and common differences, but do not make the change exogenous.

The frozen test uses the broad Area 2 composite and WIPO resident applications per million, source-key and hash locks, exact t-3/t/t+3 joins, a one-test p=0.01 rule, and explicit sample, variation and fold gates. A negative or partial result is retained. No policy, school, causal, position or featured credit follows from any verdict.
