# Services share and female labour-force participation: steelman

## Strongest case

An expanding services sector may create jobs whose location, hours, skills, and physical demands make paid employment accessible to more women. If that opportunity channel generalizes, a higher services share of value added at t should precede higher female labour-force participation at exact t+1 in countries omitted from the old 40-country screen.

## Best contrary case

Rising female participation can itself expand services. Education, childcare, household income, cultural norms, migration, and statistical definitions may drive both series. A services share is not a direct measure of opportunity or policy. The fixed-effects panel tests only the measured association, and a positive coefficient would not establish a labour-market intervention effect.

## Fixed decision boundary

All 40 old-spec countries are excluded. Only complete exact t to t+1 pairs in treatment years 1996–2022 with at least five years per country enter. A positive services-share coefficient at two-sided country-clustered p ≤ 0.10/3 supports the narrowed association; a negative coefficient at the same threshold refutes it; other estimable results are partial. Coverage below 2,500 pairs or 100 countries, or invalid estimation, is inconclusive. The old supported contemporaneous proxy result was seen and carries no prospective credit.

Some WDI/WGI vintages and eligible countries overlap already fitted IESET v2 tests. This sample is held out from the named old services-participation run, not from the entire programme. The three-test family rule covers only this new cohort.
