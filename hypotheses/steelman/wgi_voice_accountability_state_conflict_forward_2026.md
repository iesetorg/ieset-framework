# Steelman — voice and accountability and later state-based conflict

The narrow proposed mechanism is that greater room for citizens to organize, contest leadership, and hold leaders accountable can help states resolve disputes before they become organized armed conflict. The predeclared lag, prior conflict controls, income controls, and country/anchor effects make this more specific than a cross-country democracy ranking.

A strong alternative is that rapid political opening destabilizes bargaining arrangements, or that prior peace creates the measured voice improvement. A robust positive coefficient would refute the registered negative direction. WGI is a perception-based estimate and the outcome omits non-state or one-sided violence; an apparent null can also reflect its coarse incidence measure. Repression could reduce reported state-based conflict while worsening broader human safety.

A gate-cleared result remains a descriptive calibration. It cannot attribute peace or violence to a reform, movement, policy, or school of thought. The one-test p threshold does not adjust archive-wide selection.
