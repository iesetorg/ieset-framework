# Steelman: trade openness and secondary-school gross enrollment ratio holdout

The strongest version of this narrow claim is that cross-border exchange may help countries gain inputs, ideas, and competitive pressure that coincide with a higher secondary-school gross enrollment ratio in the following year. This test asks whether the within-country association appears in a country pool completely omitted from the older study, after common calendar shocks, income, and rule of law are accounted for. The old 40-country result was PARTIAL and is disclosed in the new specification.

The strongest rival explanation is: Education budgets, demographics, and compulsory-schooling rules can change enrollment and trade at the same time. Gross enrollment is not education quality. Changes in a trade-to-GDP ratio can also reflect GDP denominator movements, commodity prices, exchange rates, and global demand. Country and year effects do not resolve these problems.

The frozen endpoint is **world_bank_wdi:SE.SEC.ENRR**, read as percent of the official secondary-school-age population, at exact calendar t+1. It does not operationalise the old named mechanism. The original 40 countries are excluded; the sample, source hashes, threshold, and nine-test family are fixed before estimation. A favorable result would support a measured association only; an adverse significant result would refute its stated direction, and a weak estimate would remain PARTIAL.
