# Steelman: regulatory quality and net migration per resident

Regulatory quality can accompany opportunities that attract migrants. This test pairs WGI regulatory quality at t with net migration in the following year per 1,000 residents, using a pinned same-year population denominator and controls for real income and rule of law.

Migration also follows conflict, family networks, demographics, border policy, and reporting changes. The WGI score is not a housing-supply reform and net migration is not a welfare measure. A positive relation would establish only a measured association.

The old 40-country PARTIAL result is disclosed and its entire roster is excluded. The fixed four-test correction, sample gate, and symmetric adverse-direction rule apply. No school claim receives credit.
