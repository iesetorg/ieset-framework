# Steelman: business R&D intensity and later resident patent filings

Business enterprises may turn R&D activity into inventions for which residents
file patent applications. A larger business-sector R&D share of GDP could
therefore precede faster growth in resident filing intensity.

The indicator counts applications, not unique inventions, patent quality,
commercial value, or welfare. Firms can increase R&D after a patenting boom;
both measures can respond to sector composition, GDP, filing rules, and common
macroeconomic shocks. Population estimates affect the denominator. A positive
country-panel association would not identify the effect of public R&D policy
or private R&D spending; a null or opposite-sign result remains reportable.

This question was selected after the same R&D vintage's tertiary-employment
screen returned PARTIAL (p=0.507052). The present narrower outcome is distinct
from older WDI total-R&D patent candidates but shares data families, so it is
not independent replication or a school/policy verdict.
