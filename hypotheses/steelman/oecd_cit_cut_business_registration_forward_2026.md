# Steelman: combined statutory corporate income-tax cuts and next-year formal registrations

A lower statutory tax rate could change the expected after-tax return from starting a formal company. The frozen directional question is narrower: is a ≥1 percentage-point cut in year t associated with faster growth in newly registered limited-liability firms from t to t+1, compared with tax-stable years after separately coding hikes? Large valid reforms remain included.

The OECD rate is a headline statutory measure. Lower headline corporate tax may increase after-tax returns to formal incorporation, though the rate is not an effective tax and cannot establish reform effects. World Bank `IC.BUS.NREG` counts new limited-liability registrations; it does not measure all businesses, survival, jobs, or ownership. Country registry coverage, processing systems, business-cycle shocks, demographic growth, and anticipation can move registrations without a tax effect.

The exact sample, two tax event indicators, fixed effects, complete-case/event floors, concentration gates, meaningful +0.05 log-point cut coefficient, and two-sided Bonferroni p≤0.005 are frozen before the WDI outcome values and missingness are read. Negative, null, positive, missing, and inconclusive results must be retained. This is descriptive calibration with no causal, policy, school, or featured credit.
