# Steelman: Difficulty making ends meet after household debt-service change

A higher household debt-service ratio commits more household income to principal and interest. That squeeze could later raise the adverse-oriented OECD measure: difficulty making ends meet. The predeclared timing, exact country-year roster and four-test correction give this narrow pattern a genuine opportunity to fail.

The strongest alternative is that The measure is perceived financial strain, so macro shocks and survey changes may move it independently of household debt service. Rising DSR can also reflect higher incomes, changing rates, housing booms or borrower composition. The aggregate country series never identifies whether the people represented by the outcome have debts. A reverse sign, weak association, failed sample gate, or fragile inference must remain visible. Neither a positive association nor a null establishes a policy effect, welfare mechanism, or school-level claim.
