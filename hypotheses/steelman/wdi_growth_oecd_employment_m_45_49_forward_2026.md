# Steelman: Men ages 45–49 employment response to GDP growth

A positive coefficient could arise because expanding output raises labour demand and employment in this population over the following year. The age band and sex are an explicit, nonoverlapping cell of the labour-force population; the shared 20-cell family tests heterogeneity while accounting for its multiplicity.

The strongest rival explanation is a common business cycle: employment, output, wages, schooling, and participation can move together, and year/country effects plus one lag do not isolate a causal path. Changes in OECD survey definitions, demographic composition inside ages 45–49, migration, and pandemic-era shocks can alter the rate. A national GDP series may not reflect the job opportunities of men ages 45–49. Annual age bands are repeated cross sections, not the same people followed over time.

A negative coefficient of at least 0.10 employment percentage point per one GDP-growth percentage point, with the frozen two-sided p≤0.0005 and every method gate passing, refutes the directed claim. A weaker, imprecise, or mixed association is PARTIAL; a source, sample, influence, rank, or uncertainty failure is INCONCLUSIVE. The exact published result, including an unfavorable one, must be retained. No policy, causal, school, position, or featured credit is available.
