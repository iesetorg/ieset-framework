# Steelman: pulses relative price and later yield

A rise in the farm-gate price of pulses relative to the overall agriculture index can improve expected returns to producing that group. Farmers may respond through intensification or composition, so the frozen prediction is positive later yield growth. The design compares a country with itself, removes common calendar-year shifts and adjusts for prior outcome growth.

The strongest alternative is joint determination: a poor harvest can raise current prices, while future weather, trade restrictions, input costs, subsidies, exchange rates and land limits can affect both price and output. Group-level yield can rise through commodity mix changes. A null or negative result weakens this predictive association but says little about a causal price response.

This is one of eight correlated pre-registered crop-group × supply-margin cells. All use a two-sided 0.01/8 family cutoff and explicit sample/failure gates. Raw source archives are hash-pinned in a local cache, with derived-parquet custody limitations. Zero causal, policy, school, position and featured credit.
