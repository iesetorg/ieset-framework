# Steelman: government_consumption_investment_share_holdout_v2

Among countries excluded from the old government-consumption screen, higher government final consumption as a share of GDP at year t predicts lower gross capital formation as a share of GDP at exact year t+1, conditional on real income per person and country and treatment-year effects.

Current public consumption can compete with capital spending and private saving, while public services can also complement capital formation.

Government final consumption is not total state allocation; gross capital formation includes public investment and both ratios share a GDP denominator. Country and treatment-year effects plus baseline income leave confounding and possible reverse causation. A result in either direction is a measured association, not a policy effect.

The old SUPPORTED result was already known; all 40 countries in that historical specification are excluded. The expected sign is fixed by the claim above. Support and refutation use the same two-sided 0.10/11 family threshold after a 400-pair/35-country complete-case gate and five pairs per country. Feasibility selection inspected null masks only, without outcome magnitudes or fits. No school claim receives score credit.
