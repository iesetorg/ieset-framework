# Steelman: 2025 US state wind/solar and coal generation

The strongest alternative is that additional wind and solar generation can
coincide with flat or rising coal generation when total electricity demand
grows. A state may import or export power, change gas output, retire or start
other capacity, or experience unusual weather. Annual fuel generation by state
does not identify the marginal generator displaced by an additional MWh of
wind or solar. Even a large fraction of coal declines therefore cannot prove
causal displacement or a policy mechanism.

The old OWID country-share result through 2024 was REFUTED and stays that way.
Its countries, shares, and long endpoints differ from this fixed 39-state EIA
2024-to-2025 MWh sign check. The new 2025 endpoint extends calendar coverage,
but the 2024 baseline overlaps the old calendar window. The EIA source vintage
already existed when this new ID was registered; selected generation values
were not inspected while fixing the roster or cutoffs. The EIA data may later
be revised.

The most direct contrary result is fewer than 40% coal declines among states
whose wind-plus-solar generation rose. A result between 40% and 60% is mixed.
Fewer than 20 qualifying states or any invalid selected cell makes the result
inconclusive. There is no state substitution after values are read, and no
causal, school-score, or old-hypothesis graduation credit for any verdict.
