# Steelman: nominal_eer_trade_open_inflation_holdout_v2

In the fixed 20-country sample excluded from the old real-EER screen, a larger annual appreciation of the BIS nominal broad effective exchange-rate index at year t predicts lower consumer-price inflation at exact year t+1 more strongly where trade (exports plus imports) is a larger share of GDP at t, conditional on inflation and real GDP growth at t and country and treatment-year effects.

A stronger currency can lower import prices, and a larger trade share may increase the exposure of consumer prices to those prices. A nominal rather than CPI-adjusted EER avoids including domestic inflation in the predictor by construction.

A stronger currency can also reflect disinflationary monetary policy or changes in demand. Trade as a share of GDP includes exports and is not import content of the consumer basket. Euro-area countries share a currency, and country clustering may not capture common shocks. The t to t+1 lag may miss same-year pass-through.

The historical real-EER interaction was PARTIAL, with coefficient -0.0009935 and p=0.257, and was known before this registration. All 42 historically eligible countries are excluded; the 20-country follow-up roster is fixed from source keys. Screening also saw null masks, without held-out values or fits. A negative interaction at two-sided p<=0.01 with 400 complete observations, all 20 countries, and at least five years each supports this descriptive association; a positive one at the same threshold refutes it. Other outcomes are PARTIAL or INCONCLUSIVE under the frozen rule. No school or policy score credit follows.
