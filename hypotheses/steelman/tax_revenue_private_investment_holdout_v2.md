# Steelman: tax_revenue_private_investment_holdout_v2

Among countries excluded from the old redistribution-tax screen, higher total tax revenue as a share of GDP at year t predicts lower private gross capital formation as a share of GDP at exact year t+1, conditional on real income per person and country and treatment-year effects.

A larger tax take might lower private after-tax returns, while fiscal capacity can strengthen the institutions and infrastructure that attract investment.

Total tax revenue does not measure redistribution or marginal tax rates; private gross capital formation and tax receipts both have GDP in the denominator. Country and treatment-year effects plus baseline income leave confounding and possible reverse causation. A result in either direction is a measured association, not a policy effect.

The old REFUTED result was already known; all 53 countries in that historical specification are excluded. The expected sign is fixed by the claim above. Support and refutation use the same two-sided 0.10/11 family threshold after a 400-pair/35-country complete-case gate and five pairs per country. Feasibility selection inspected null masks only, without outcome magnitudes or fits. No school claim receives score credit.
