# Private credit depth and TFP growth: steelman

## Strongest case

Access to private credit can finance productive firms, adoption of technology, and movement of capital toward higher-return uses. A genuinely broad financial market could therefore precede faster measured total-factor-productivity growth. The new test uses private credit as a share of GDP at t and the exact next-year change in the PWT TFP index, in countries excluded from the old 40-country GDP-growth screen.

## Best contrary case

Credit depth can reflect property cycles, household borrowing, or leverage rather than better capital allocation. Stronger TFP can also attract credit, and PWT TFP is a modeled residual with revisions and heterogeneous measurement. Country and year effects plus income and rule-of-law controls do not isolate a credit-supply shock. Even a supported result is an association, not proof of the old reallocative mechanism.

## Fixed decision boundary

All 40 old-spec countries are excluded. Only finite, positive exact t and t+1 PWT index values in treatment years 1996–2018 enter, with at least five complete years per country. A positive credit-depth coefficient at two-sided country-clustered p ≤ 0.10/3 supports the narrowed association; a negative coefficient at the same threshold refutes it; other estimable results are partial. Coverage below 1,000 pairs or 50 countries, or invalid estimation, is inconclusive. The old supported GDP-growth proxy result was seen and carries no prospective credit.

Some WDI/WGI vintages and eligible countries overlap already fitted IESET v2 tests. This sample is held out from the named old financial-depth run, not from the entire programme. The three-test family rule covers only this new cohort.
