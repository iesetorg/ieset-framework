# Steelman: annual precipitation departure and later Tomatoes producer prices

Large annual precipitation departures can disrupt harvests or anticipated supply and raise subsequent farm-gate prices. The frozen positive prediction is tested within countries, removing common exposure years and controlling prior price growth and the other weather departure.

The strongest alternative is that national annual climate averages miss tomato growing regions and seasons. Rainfall surplus can help dry areas, and warmth can benefit cooler areas. Prices also move with inflation, exchange rates, imports, demand, policy, energy, storage, crop substitution and FAO estimation. Even a positive, family-significant coefficient would be descriptive, not causal.

Both correlated cells share one two-sided 0.01/2 cutoff, strict source and sample gates, and zero causal, policy, school, position or featured credit.
