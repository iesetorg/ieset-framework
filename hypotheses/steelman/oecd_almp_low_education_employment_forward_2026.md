# Steelman: ALMP spending changes and low-education employment

Public employment services, training, and hiring support can be especially relevant to adults with low formal education. If additional spending improves matching or skills, an increase in public active labour market programme (ALMP) expenditure may precede a larger rise in their employment rate than a spending decrease. The deliberately narrow prediction is an association between budget-share categories and the next annual change in the measured Eurostat rate.

The strongest rival is reverse response: governments expand ALMP budgets when low-education employment is deteriorating. Spending as a share of GDP also changes when its denominator falls, and the SOCX total says nothing about take-up, targeting, programme design, or implementation. A low-education rate can move with education classification and workforce composition. Country and year effects, baseline employment, and income controls do not remove these problems.

This one-test calibration compares SOCX TP60 changes of at least +0.05 GDP percentage point with changes of at most −0.05, with smaller changes retained as the stable reference. The primary contrast is the increase coefficient minus the decrease coefficient. A two-sided p ≤ 0.01 and an absolute contrast of at least 0.5 employment-rate percentage point are required for a directional verdict. An imprecise or smaller contrast is PARTIAL, and an opposite precise contrast is REFUTED. A result cannot establish that programmes caused employment or support policy, school, position, or featured credit.
