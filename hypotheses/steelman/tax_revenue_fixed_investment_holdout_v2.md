# Steelman: tax_revenue_fixed_investment_holdout_v2

Among countries excluded from the old sector-neutral-tax screen, higher total tax revenue as a share of GDP at year t predicts lower gross fixed capital formation as a share of GDP at exact year t+1, conditional on real income per person and country and treatment-year effects.

A larger tax take might reduce funds available for capital formation, while revenue can finance infrastructure and other complements to investment.

Total tax revenue does not measure tax neutrality or exemptions; the two ratios share GDP and fixed investment includes public as well as private capital. Country and treatment-year effects plus baseline income leave confounding and possible reverse causation. A result in either direction is a measured association, not a policy effect.

The old PARTIAL result was already known; all 30 countries in that historical specification are excluded. The expected sign is fixed by the claim above. Support and refutation use the same two-sided 0.10/11 family threshold after a 400-pair/35-country complete-case gate and five pairs per country. Feasibility selection inspected null masks only, without outcome magnitudes or fits. No school claim receives score credit.
