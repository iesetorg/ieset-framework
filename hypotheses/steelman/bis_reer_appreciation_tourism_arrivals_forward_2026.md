# Steelman: real appreciation and inbound tourist arrivals

A sustained real appreciation can raise destination prices faced by foreign visitors.
When those visitors can substitute toward other countries, international arrivals
may grow more slowly in the following year. The BIS broad real effective exchange
rate supplies a consistently defined, observed exchange-rate and relative-price
series, and WDI records inbound arrival trips. Requiring a substantial five-log-point
appreciation avoids treating trivial changes as an event.

The most serious objection is that the BIS basket weights **manufacturing trade**
partners, not the countries from which tourists arrive. Domestic prices paid by
visitors, airfares, visa access, safety, accommodation capacity, and tourism
marketing can move differently from the CPI-adjusted REER. A currency can
appreciate because the destination became more productive or attractive; an
arrivals increase could therefore accompany appreciation. Recessions and crises
can change exchange rates and travel simultaneously. Country and year effects,
GDP growth, and baseline arrivals do not eliminate these common causes.

WDI's arrival count can use border counts, surveys, accommodation records, or
broader visitor counts when overnight-tourist figures are missing. National
reporting breaks and destination-specific COVID responses cannot be repaired
from the pinned source's blank status flags. The outcome window ends in 2019
to avoid the pandemic, but earlier shocks remain. A strong coefficient would
be a descriptive pricing-pattern calibration only. It would not identify a
devaluation policy effect, tourism welfare gain, or a school of thought's success.
The preregistered sample, material-event, concentration, and uncertainty gates
can yield INCONCLUSIVE or PARTIAL results without any post-result repair.
