# Steelman: precipitation departures and cereals, primary yield

Aggregate cereals, primary production can be disrupted when annual precipitation departs far from a country's 1961–1990 normal. The frozen directional prediction is lower within-year yield growth. The design compares each country with itself, removes common calendar years, and controls prior crop growth and the other weather-departure measure.

A strong alternative is that country-wide annual weather averages do not describe the crop's growing locations or season. Precipitation surpluses may help dry areas; warmer years may help cold areas. Irrigation, planting decisions, crop composition, pests, reporting estimates and long-run adaptation can also dominate. A negative, null or positive coefficient is an association, not a causal crop response.
