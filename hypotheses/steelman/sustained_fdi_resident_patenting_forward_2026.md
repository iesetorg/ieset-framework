# Sustained FDI inflows and later resident patent filings

## Best case for the claim

Foreign investors can bring processes, engineers, supplier requirements, and competitive pressure that local firms learn from. A country with several consecutive years of substantial net FDI inflows may therefore see more resident patent applications over the following three years. The fixed exposure is a three-year mean of net FDI inflows at least 3% of GDP, so the comparison does not hinge on one extreme annual flow. The endpoint counts applications by residents per million people, with exact t and t+3 measurements.

## Strongest countercase

Balance-of-payments FDI includes acquisitions, retained earnings, affiliate debt, and financial routing; it is not a measure of greenfield factories or technology transfer. An innovative destination may attract FDI, rather than become innovative because of it. Foreign affiliates may patent through a parent company abroad, so local resident filings can stay flat even when real learning occurs. IP-law changes, patent-office coverage, reporting gaps, GDP-denominator movements, population estimates, and country shocks can move this association without changing invention. Country and year effects and two GDP controls do not resolve those confounders. The positive sign is consequently a narrow descriptive prediction.

## Frozen falsification

The preregistered test uses treatment years 2004–2019 in the fixed 2004 population-at-least-five-million cohort. It compares the t-to-t+3 change in log(1 + resident patent applications per million) for 3%-plus and below-3% three-year FDI means, controlling for baseline patent intensity, log real GDP per person, real GDP growth, and country and treatment-year effects. Country-cluster CR1 inference uses a two-sided Student t with G−1 degrees of freedom. A positive coefficient of at least 0.05 log points and p≤0.01 supports the directional association; a negative coefficient of at least 0.05 log points in magnitude with p≤0.01 refutes it. Other estimable results are partial. Frozen sample, within-country switcher, and residualized-treatment concentration gates can force an inconclusive verdict before significance is read as support.

The 3% event threshold was chosen while blind to patent values, nulls, and statuses. With continuous FDI-share exposure, the top 1% of rows accounted for 44.8% of residualized treatment variation in the treatment/control-only preflight. The 3% event reduced that concentration to 8.8% without removing or winsorizing FDI observations. The event threshold must not be changed after patent access.

## Credit boundary

This is one selected association in a much larger hypothesis archive. It cannot receive causal, policy, position, school-score, or featured credit, even if its local threshold is met. It does not test an FDI liberalization, investment treaty, tax policy, or patent quality.

WIPO explicitly warns that missing patent-office entries may be zero or truly missing; this test never fills them with zero.
