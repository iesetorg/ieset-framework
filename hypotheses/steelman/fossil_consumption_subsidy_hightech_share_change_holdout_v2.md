# Steelman: fossil_consumption_subsidy_hightech_share_change_holdout_v2

In the fixed 22-country sample excluded from the old export-value fit, a higher OWID/IEA explicit fossil-fuel consumption subsidy amount per resident in year t predicts a smaller t-to-t+1 change in WDI high-technology exports as a percentage of manufactured exports, conditional on real GDP per person at t and country and treatment-year effects.

One plausible interpretation is that an energy price gap favors established energy-intensive activities and is associated with slower shifts in the manufactured export mix. The registered outcome tests only that mix as measured by WDI. It cannot show how any firm responds, whether total high-tech exports rise or fall, or whether a country's industrial capability changes.

The opposite sign is credible. Subsidies may accompany industrial expansion, or oil and gas receipts may simultaneously finance subsidies and export investment. Global fuel prices can move IEA price-gap estimates without a change in domestic policy. A high-tech share can rise when other manufactured exports fall. Country and year effects and a GDP-per-person control cannot remove all time-varying confounding. The selected 22-country roster, dominated by resource exporters, is not representative of all economies; about 20 country clusters give limited power.

The old test used high-tech export value and returned PARTIAL with a positive coefficient of +0.030319 (p=0.224323, 169 observations, 21 countries). This result was seen before the new design. The old 21-country roster is wholly excluded, but the old WITS export-value and new WDI export-share measures share the World Bank/WITS trade-data family, so this is not independent source replication. Source years and vintages can still overlap unrelated IESET tests. Selection inspected country/year keys and value null masks only, without held-out magnitudes, signs, or fits. A negative coefficient at two-sided p<=0.01 after the fixed 200-pair, 20-country, five-years-per-country gates supports only the descriptive claim; a positive one at that threshold refutes it. Other outcomes are PARTIAL or INCONCLUSIVE. No causal, welfare, policy, or school credit follows.
