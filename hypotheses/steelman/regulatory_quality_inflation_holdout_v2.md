# Steelman: regulatory_quality_inflation_holdout_v2

Among countries excluded from the old price-control screen, a higher World Governance Indicators regulatory-quality estimate at year t predicts lower transformed consumer-price inflation at exact year t+1, conditional on real income per person and country and treatment-year effects.

Clearer and more predictable regulation might ease some supply frictions, while monetary, exchange-rate, fiscal, and commodity shocks can dominate inflation.

Regulatory quality is not a price-control law, inflation is not a direct shortage measure, and the asinh transform limits extreme-inflation influence without discarding observations. Country and treatment-year effects plus baseline income leave confounding and possible reverse causation. A result in either direction is a measured association, not a policy effect.

The old PARTIAL result was already known; all 40 countries in that historical specification are excluded. The expected sign is fixed by the claim above. Support and refutation use the same two-sided 0.10/11 family threshold after a 400-pair/35-country complete-case gate and five pairs per country. Feasibility selection inspected null masks only, without outcome magnitudes or fits. No school claim receives score credit.
