# Steelman: foreign market potential and later trade openness

A country closer to growing foreign markets may find more firms able to cover
fixed export costs and import more intermediate goods. If market opportunity
shows up before a rise in trade relative to GDP, that is a measurable
descriptive pattern. A positive coefficient under the frozen family rule is
the strongest result this design can support.

The rival view is strong: CEPII partner GDP is in current dollars, so exchange
rates, price levels and coverage shifts can move the exposure. Domestic GDP
is the denominator of WDI openness, and local cycles, ports, supply chains,
institutions and policy can jointly affect both sides. Country and year
effects plus lag ordering do not remove these confounds. Null or opposite
estimates count fully; no treaty or welfare inference follows.
