# Steelman: bargaining coverage and the 2025 unemployment holdout

Collective-bargaining coverage may coordinate wages, training, hours, and adjustment across firms, which can absorb shocks without raising unemployment. It can also compress wage differences while preserving employment through sector agreements. The 2023 coverage measure precedes the 2025 endpoint, and controlling for the 2023 unemployment rate removes some persistent cross-country level differences.

The strongest objection is that 26 countries and one later year cannot identify the effect of bargaining institutions. Coverage is endogenous to industry mix, union law, unemployment, and historical institutions. Countries experienced different 2023–25 demand, migration, inflation, and policy shocks. A common level of 2023 unemployment cannot remove all of these differences. The later LFS vintage may revise the baseline as well as add 2025. The one-sided 0.2 percentage-point margin per ten coverage points is a narrow descriptive non-inferiority rule, not proof of zero effect.

A method-valid result can calibrate only the measured 2023 coverage to 2025 working-age unemployment association. A failed completeness or influence gate is inconclusive. Any result leaves the archived same-commit claim and its inconsistent old verdict artifacts untouched and grants zero causal, school, policy, position, or featured credit.
