# Steelman: public education spending and female prime-age participation

Education spending may support skills and social infrastructure that help
women enter or remain in the labour force. A larger rise in total government
education expenditure as a share of GDP during 2005–2015 might therefore
precede faster growth in female age-25–54 labour-force participation during
2015–2023. The model controls the 2005 spending share, the 2015 participation
level, and the 2005–2015 participation trend.

The spending indicator covers all education, not women-specific programmes;
its ratio can rise when GDP weakens. ILO participation includes people
seeking work and can be modelled; it does not count job quality or earnings.
Female participation also responds to childcare, household incomes,
social norms, fertility, labour demand, and shocks. A positive cross-country
association would not show that an education budget change caused it.

The fixed sample, variation, influence, and leave-one-out gates can return
INCONCLUSIVE. Opposite and null results remain reportable. This is a
descriptive screen with zero causal, policy, school, position, or featured
credit, despite the fiscal classification of its exposure.
