# Steelman: trade openness and household consumption holdout

The strongest version of this limited claim is that access to imports,
export markets, and competition raises the quantity or quality of household
consumption. A country and year fixed-effects panel in countries omitted from
the original study can test whether that within-country association survives
income and institutional controls. The old result was partial, so an adverse
or null holdout result is just as informative as a favorable one.

The strongest rival explanation is that richer or faster-growing economies
both trade more and consume more. Exchange rates, commodity cycles, tourism,
and trade measured as a share of GDP can move without a liberalising policy.
GDP per capita is itself close to the outcome and may absorb part of the
channel. WGI rule of law is a perception measure. These controls cannot make
the panel causal.

The frozen test includes all complete country-years outside the old 40-country
sample and uses a two-sided 0.01 decision threshold. It evaluates the exact
WDI private-consumption series, not a broad consumer-welfare concept.
