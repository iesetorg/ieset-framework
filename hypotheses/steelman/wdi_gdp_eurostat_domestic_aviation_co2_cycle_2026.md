# Steelman: GDP cycle and domestic aviation fuel combustion CO2 (CRF1A3A)

The fixed descriptive claim is that a one-percentage-point higher WDI real GDP growth rate in year `t` is associated with at least +0.005 log-point greater same-year direct fuel-combustion CO2 growth in CRF category `CRF1A3A`, from `t−1` to `t`. The prespecified two-sided threshold is `p≤0.01/12`, with all twelve mutually non-nested source sectors retained in the family denominator. A negative coefficient of equal magnitude and significance refutes the directional claim; an estimable weaker result is partial.

National output growth can raise energy demand, freight, travel, or production, making this sector-specific emissions response plausible. The strongest rival account is: Domestic routes, air traffic rules, aircraft efficiency, and substitution with rail can move domestic-aviation fuel CO2. International aviation is outside this selected code. GDP growth measures the whole economy, while this CRF category records territorial source emissions, not a NACE industry output or consumption footprint. A shared energy-price or policy shock can affect both. Country and calendar-year effects remove stable differences and common shocks, not time-varying local confounding.

The Eurostat Parquet retains no cell flags, although EEA/Eurostat metadata explain that a zero can mean not occurring or included elsewhere. The frozen log analysis therefore excludes nonpositive endpoints without treating them as verified structural zeros. Exact years, source hashes, sample floors, concentration gates, and uncertainty are frozen before outcome values or nulls are accessed.

This is one of twelve related, archive-selected sector calibrations. It is not a policy intervention, independent replication, or causal test. Every verdict has zero causal, policy, school-score, position, and featured credit.
