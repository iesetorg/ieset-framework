# Minimum-wage MEDIAN ratio and youth unemployment, 2020–2024

## Strongest case for the claim

A higher statutory minimum relative to the wage distribution can make entry-level hiring more costly. Youth workers have shorter experience and may be more exposed to that margin. Country and year effects remove stable country differences and common annual shocks; same-year real GDP growth controls one measured part of country-specific demand. A positive within-country coefficient in a period wholly after the old fit would be a fresh descriptive signal.

## Strongest case against the claim

A wage floor can raise retention, search quality, and household demand, or firms can adjust hours, prices, profits, and vacancies without increasing unemployment. Youth unemployment is a modelled labor-force rate, not a direct employment count. Policy can respond to labor-market conditions, while COVID recovery, inflation, and migration differ across countries. Five annual observations per country cannot isolate a policy mechanism. The OECD file has both MEAN and MEDIAN rows, and its generic measure/unit labels say “average wages”; the registered analysis therefore names its exact MEDIAN aggregation rather than silently blending rows or overstating denominator semantics.

## What would change our view

The registered primary coefficient must be positive at the family two-sided cutoff of 0.01 and remain nonnegative when 2020 is removed. A stable, significant negative coefficient refutes the stated positive association. All other identifiable outcomes are partial; missing coverage or an invalid estimator is inconclusive. No outcome from this narrower test repairs the old youth-versus-total comparison or earns school-score or causal credit.

## Source and custody boundary

The fixed 32-country roster and 2020–2024 window came from OECD and ILO categorical keys alone. The exact OECD, ILO, and WDI source bytes and their tracked publisher manifest are hash-pinned in the specification and runner before any candidate value or null mask is read. This is temporal calibration with possible country overlap, not a country holdout.
