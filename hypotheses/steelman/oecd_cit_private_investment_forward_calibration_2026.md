# OECD corporate-tax cuts and private investment: steelman

A lower combined statutory corporate-income-tax rate can raise the after-tax return to a marginal capital project. Fixed investment may rise after the tax change if firms have viable projects and access to financing. The preregistered direction is a larger two-year change in **private gross fixed capital formation as a share of GDP** after a cut of at least one percentage point than after a tax-stable year.

The strongest objection is endogeneity: governments often cut rates in response to weak investment, and investors may anticipate reforms. The statutory rate omits tax bases, allowances, loss offsets, and tax holidays. Private investment as a GDP share can move because GDP changes, and the two-year window cannot establish a higher capital stock per worker. Country and year effects, baseline investment, and a separate hike indicator narrow descriptive confounding but do not remove it. A null or contrary coefficient must be retained. The archived `corporate_tax_rate_investment_elasticity` placeholder is not repaired by this new, narrower test.

The familywise rule is two-sided p≤0.005 and a practical magnitude of at least 0.5 percentage point for either direction, after the frozen sample and influence floors. This is descriptive calibration only, with zero causal or scoring credit.
