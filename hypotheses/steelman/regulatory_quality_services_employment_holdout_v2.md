# Steelman: regulatory_quality_services_employment_holdout_v2

Among countries excluded from the old sectoral-reallocation screen, a higher World Governance Indicators regulatory-quality estimate at year t predicts a higher services share of employment at exact year t+1, conditional on real income per person and country and treatment-year effects.

Predictable rules may ease entry by service firms, while income growth and structural change can drive both perceptions of regulatory quality and service employment.

Regulatory-quality perceptions are not observed price signals, and a services employment share does not measure reallocation speed or job quality. Country and treatment-year effects plus baseline income leave confounding and possible reverse causation. A result in either direction is a measured association, not a policy effect.

The old PARTIAL result was already known; all 30 countries in that historical specification are excluded. The expected sign is fixed by the claim above. Support and refutation use the same two-sided 0.10/11 family threshold after a 400-pair/35-country complete-case gate and five pairs per country. Feasibility selection inspected null masks only, without outcome magnitudes or fits. No school claim receives score credit.
