# Steelman: 2019 statutory corporate tax and CbCR profit booking

The strongest interpretation is a **risk screen**: jurisdictions with lower
combined statutory corporate-income-tax rates may record more multinational
profit per local employee because the location of taxable profit is more
responsive than the location of labour. OECD CbCR aggregates report profits
and employees by tax jurisdiction and therefore measure the proposed
misalignment more directly than FDI flows or GDP growth.

The rival account is substantial. High profit per employee can reflect
genuine capital intensity, intellectual-property production, sector mix,
resource rents, a small workforce, or the changing set of multinational
parents that submitted CbCR reports. A statutory rate can also differ sharply
from the effective rate facing a particular enterprise. The local derived
extract lacks the raw OECD response, so its source-native currency multiplier
cannot be reconstructed; ranks are used to avoid a dollar-magnitude claim.
The 2019 cross-section cannot distinguish those channels or identify an
effect of a tax reform.

The fixed falsifier is a material positive rank association under the same
sample and permutation gate. A weak or unstable association is PARTIAL.
Neither result licenses a global shifted-profit estimate, a treatment effect,
or any school or policy score.
