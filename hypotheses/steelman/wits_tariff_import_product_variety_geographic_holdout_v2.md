# wits_tariff_import_product_variety_geographic_holdout_v2: steelman

## Strongest case

A higher applied merchandise tariff raises the cost of importing marginal products. A country-disjoint test of the next year's imported HS6 product-line count is a direct, narrower check of whether this measured relationship travels beyond the old fit.

## Best contrary case

Tariffs may respond to domestic shocks, and the product-line count moves with demand, customs reporting, and HS revisions. Import variety need not map to consumer welfare. Country and year effects do not identify a policy effect.

## Frozen decision boundary

The old PARTIAL result was known before registration. All 39 countries in the old specification are excluded. Only exact t and t+1 observations from source-pinned vintages count. At least five years per country, 500 complete pairs, and 40 countries are required. This two-test family uses a two-sided p<=0.05 threshold for both negative support and positive refutation; otherwise an estimable result is PARTIAL, and a failed gate is INCONCLUSIVE. No result exists at registration. Any result is descriptive calibration with zero school credit.
