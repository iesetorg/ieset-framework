# Steelman: agricultural employment transition with services productivity

## Strongest case

Moving workers out of low-productivity agriculture can lift output per person when expanding services absorb them productively. Direct measures of agriculture's employment share and real services value added per services worker address the archived mechanism more closely than a services GDP-share proxy.

## Strongest alternative

The transition can reflect income growth rather than cause it, and services productivity can rise because worker counts fall or sector prices and classifications change. The WDI/ILOSTAT workforce construction combines source conventions. A later growth endpoint and prior-growth control reduce same-period accounting overlap but do not identify causality.

## Falsifiers and limits

A precise adverse interaction or adverse slope in productivity-rising economies refutes the narrow forward prediction. Thin groups, weak agricultural-share variation, unstable leverage, or missing endpoints are inconclusive. The manufacturing branch and the archived contemporaneous "only when" claim remain untested.
