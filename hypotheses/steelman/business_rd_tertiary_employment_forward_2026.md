# Steelman: business R&D intensity and tertiary employment rate

Business R&D can expand demand for highly educated workers and related
occupations. A higher business-sector R&D share of GDP might therefore
precede an increase in the employment rate of tertiary-educated adults.

R&D spending can also follow skilled labour supply or growth. The rate among
tertiary-educated people changes with who gains a degree, who joins the labour
force, and international mobility; it is not a count of new R&D jobs. A
positive measured association would not identify a business subsidy effect or
the return to education. A null or opposite-sign result must be retained.
